1Finite and non-finite verbs10 examples

The first verb of a verb group is finite and carries the tense; every other verb is non-finite (to + verb, φ + verb, -en form, -ing form).

  1. She has been waiting for an hour.

    finite: has · non-finite: been, waiting only the first auxiliary takes tense

  2. The work might have been finished by now.

    finite: might · non-finite: finished the modal is finite; the -en form is non-finite

  3. Rahul decided to join the army.

    to + verb to join does not change with tense

  4. Mother made me wash the dishes.

    φ + verb the base form without to

  5. I saw a boy climbing the coconut tree.

    -ing form describes what the boy was doing

  6. We found the door broken.

    -en form the third form of break

  7. Rina plans to visit Puri. → Rina planned to visit Puri.

    tense change the finite verb changes; to visit does not

  8. * Gopal going to school.

    not a sentence no finite verb → add is

  9. * The letter written yesterday.

    not a sentence no finite verb → add was

  10. The boy sitting next to me is my cousin.

    non-finite: sitting · finite: is a sentence needs one finite verb; non-finite verbs add detail

2Non-finite clauses10 examples

A non-finite clause is a clause reduced to a non-finite form. It can be opened up into a separate simple sentence.

  1. I want to pass the examination.

    to-clause = I will pass the examination + I want it.

  2. My parents want me to become a teacher.

    to-clause with its own subject = I will become a teacher + my parents want it.

  3. It is difficult to climb this hill.

    to-clause = Someone climbs this hill + it is difficult.

  4. The rain helped the farmers sow their seeds.

    φ-clause = The farmers sowed their seeds + the rain helped them.

  5. Hearing the bell, the children ran out.

    -ing clause = The children heard the bell + they ran out.

  6. Tired after the long walk, we sat down to rest.

    -en clause = We were tired after the long walk + we sat down.

  7. The man standing at the gate is a guard.

    -ing clause = The man is standing at the gate + he is a guard.

  8. The bridge built last year has already cracked.

    -en clause = The bridge was built last year + it has cracked.

  9. I asked him to wait for me.

    to-clause = He should wait for me + I asked him.

  10. She was happy to hear the good news.

    to-clause = She heard the good news + she was happy.

3What to + verb can do10 examples

to + verb can be the subject, the object, a describing word after a noun, or an adjunct of purpose.

  1. To swim in the sea is great fun.

    subject what is great fun?

  2. To read good books is a pleasure.

    subject what is a pleasure?

  3. We decided to stay at home.

    object decided what?

  4. The children love to play in the rain.

    object love what?

  5. The coach told the players to practise every day.

    object (after a noun phrase) told whom? the players; told what? to practise

  6. I have a lot of homework to do.

    describes a noun what kind of homework? work to do

  7. She was the first girl to reach the top.

    describes a noun works like an adjective

  8. Give me something to eat.

    describes a pronoun something to eat

  9. He went to the market to buy vegetables.

    adjunct (purpose) why did he go?

  10. She works hard to support her family.

    adjunct (purpose) answers why?

4φ + verb (without to)10 examples

Modals, let, make, help, have, see, hear, watch, notice, had better and would rather are followed by the base form without to.

  1. You should eat more fruit.

    modal + φ verb not should to eat

  2. Birds can fly, but penguins cannot.

    modal + φ verb can + base form

  3. Our teacher let us leave early today.

    let + object + φ verb not let us to leave

  4. The film made everyone cry.

    make + object + φ verb active make takes φ + verb

  5. Could you help me carry this bag?

    help + object + φ verb help can take φ or to + verb

  6. I will have the tailor shorten these trousers.

    have + object + φ verb getting someone to do a job

  7. We watched the sun set over the sea.

    watch + object + φ verb the whole action seen

  8. Did you hear the dog bark last night?

    hear + object + φ verb a verb of perception

  9. You had better take an umbrella.

    had better + φ verb advice

  10. I would rather walk than wait for the bus.

    would rather + φ verb preference

5to + verb after certain verbs10 examples

Three patterns: verb + to-verb (hope, decide, refuse); verb + noun phrase + to-verb (advise, allow, tell); and verbs that take either (want, expect, ask).

  1. She refused to answer my question.

    verb + to-verb refuse is followed directly by to + verb

  2. We hope to finish the project by Friday.

    verb + to-verb hope + to + verb

  3. He pretended to be asleep.

    verb + to-verb pretend + to + verb

  4. Rina managed to catch the last bus.

    verb + to-verb manage = succeed in doing

  5. The doctor advised him to rest.

    verb + noun phrase + to-verb advise someone to do something

  6. My parents allowed me to go on the trip.

    verb + noun phrase + to-verb allow someone to do something

  7. The officer ordered the soldiers to march.

    verb + noun phrase + to-verb order someone to do something

  8. I want to go home.

    want + to-verb I will go

  9. I want you to go home.

    want + noun phrase + to-verb you will go

  10. She asked to see the manager. / She asked me to see the manager.

    ask, both patterns who will see the manager changes

6verb–ing after certain verbs10 examples

Some verbs (enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, practise, keep, give up) and every preposition are followed by verb–ing; a few verbs change meaning with to + verb.

  1. I enjoy reading detective stories.

    enjoy + -ing not enjoy to read

  2. Please avoid using plastic bags.

    avoid + -ing avoid + -ing

  3. Have you finished writing your essay?

    finish + -ing finish + -ing

  4. Would you mind opening the window?

    mind + -ing a polite request

  5. My grandfather has given up smoking.

    give up + -ing a phrasal verb + -ing

  6. She kept talking during the film.

    keep + -ing continued

  7. He is good at drawing.

    preposition + -ing after at, in, of, for … use -ing

  8. Thank you for helping me.

    preposition + -ing for + -ing

  9. I remember meeting him at the fair. / Remember to post the letter.

    remember + -ing / to-verb -ing looks back to the past; to + verb looks forward to a task

  10. He stopped talking. / He stopped to talk to his friend.

    stop + -ing / to-verb -ing = no longer did it; to-verb = paused in order to do it
