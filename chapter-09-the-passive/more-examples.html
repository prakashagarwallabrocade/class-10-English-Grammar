1Present tenses in the passive10 examples

Present simple: am / is / are + V3. Present progressive: am / is / are being + V3. Present perfect: has / have been + V3.

  1. English is spoken all over the world.

    present simple People speak English all over the world.

  2. These toys are made in China.

    present simple plural subject → are

  3. The classroom is cleaned every day.

    present simple a regular action

  4. Our letters are delivered in the morning.

    present simple The postman delivers our letters …

  5. A new bridge is being built over the river.

    present progressive They are building a new bridge …

  6. The patients are being examined by the doctor.

    present progressive The doctor is examining the patients.

  7. My car is being repaired at the moment.

    present progressive going on now

  8. The results have been announced.

    present perfect They have announced the results.

  9. My bicycle has been stolen!

    present perfect a past action with a result now

  10. Five hundred trees have been planted this year.

    present perfect an unfinished time period (this year)

2Past tenses in the passive10 examples

Past simple: was / were + V3. Past progressive: was / were being + V3. Past perfect: had been + V3.

  1. The Taj Mahal was built by Shah Jahan.

    past simple Shah Jahan built the Taj Mahal.

  2. The thieves were caught last night.

    past simple The police caught the thieves …

  3. This poem was written in 1910.

    past simple a finished past time

  4. Many houses were damaged by the cyclone.

    past simple The cyclone damaged many houses.

  5. The road was being repaired when we passed.

    past progressive Workers were repairing the road …

  6. The children were being taught a new song.

    past progressive in progress at a past time

  7. The dinner had been cooked before the guests arrived.

    past perfect Mother had cooked the dinner …

  8. The patient had already been taken to hospital.

    past perfect earlier than another past action

  9. The match was won by the visiting team.

    past simple The visiting team won the match.

  10. When I reached, all the tickets had been sold.

    past perfect They had sold all the tickets.

3Passive with modals10 examples

modal + be + V3 (present / future); modal + have been + V3 (past).

  1. The work will be finished tomorrow.

    will be + V3 We will finish the work tomorrow.

  2. This medicine must be kept away from children.

    must be + V3 You must keep this medicine …

  3. Mobile phones should not be used in class.

    should be + V3 advice in the passive

  4. The problem can be solved easily.

    can be + V3 We can solve the problem easily.

  5. The meeting may be postponed.

    may be + V3 possibility

  6. Rules must be obeyed.

    must be + V3 We must obey rules.

  7. The letter ought to be posted today.

    ought to be + V3 duty

  8. The parcel should have been delivered yesterday.

    should have been + V3 it was not delivered

  9. The accident could have been avoided.

    could have been + V3 a past possibility not taken

  10. The window might have been broken by the wind.

    might have been + V3 a guess about the past

4Passive with get / have and with a complement10 examples

get + V3 for accidents or changes; have / get something + V3 when someone does a job for us; a complement stays after the verb.

  1. He got hurt in the match.

    get + V3 an accident

  2. My shirt got torn on a nail.

    get + V3 an unplanned event

  3. They got married last year.

    get + V3 a change of state

  4. I had my hair cut yesterday.

    have + object + V3 the barber cut it

  5. We are having our house painted.

    have + object + V3 painters are doing it

  6. You should get your eyes tested.

    get + object + V3 an optician will test them

  7. She had her purse stolen on the bus.

    have + object + V3 something bad that happened to her

  8. He was elected captain.

    passive + complement They elected him captain.

  9. The baby was named Rohan.

    passive + complement They named the baby Rohan.

  10. The door was painted green.

    passive + complement Someone painted the door green.

5Verbs with two objects10 examples

With give, send, show, tell, offer, teach either object can become the subject; the person is more usual.

  1. I was given a watch on my birthday.

    person as subject They gave me a watch …

  2. A watch was given to me on my birthday.

    thing as subject add to before the person

  3. The students were shown the new laboratory.

    person as subject The principal showed the students …

  4. The new laboratory was shown to the students.

    thing as subject to + person

  5. She was offered a job in Bengaluru.

    person as subject They offered her a job …

  6. We were taught English by Mrs Panda.

    person as subject Mrs Panda taught us English.

  7. The boy was told a story by his grandmother.

    person as subject His grandmother told the boy a story.

  8. A letter was sent to the headmaster.

    thing as subject They sent the headmaster a letter.

  9. The winners will be presented with prizes.

    present … with The chief guest will present prizes to the winners.

  10. I have been promised a seat on the bus.

    person as subject They have promised me a seat.

6Passive with verbs of reporting10 examples

Two patterns: It is said / believed / reported that … and Subject + is said / believed + to-infinitive.

  1. It is said that the castle is haunted.

    It is said that People say that the castle is haunted.

  2. The castle is said to be haunted.

    subject + is said to the same meaning

  3. It is believed that he is very rich.

    It is believed that People believe that …

  4. He is believed to be very rich.

    subject + is believed to the same meaning

  5. It was reported that ten people were injured.

    It was reported that news reports

  6. Ten people were reported to have been injured.

    subject + perfect infinitive the injury happened before the report

  7. It is known that smoking causes cancer.

    It is known that a general fact

  8. The thief is thought to have escaped to Kolkata.

    subject + perfect infinitive People think that the thief has escaped …

  9. It is expected that the prices will fall.

    It is expected that people expect

  10. She is expected to win the election.

    subject + is expected to a future action

7Passive infinitive and -ing forms10 examples

to be + V3 and being + V3 are the passive forms of to + verb and verb-ing.

  1. I want to be invited to the party.

    to be + V3 I want them to invite me.

  2. This letter needs to be signed by the principal.

    to be + V3 someone must sign it

  3. The children hate being teased.

    being + V3 they hate it when others tease them

  4. She does not like being kept waiting.

    being + V3 after like

  5. The house is to be sold.

    be to be + V3 a plan or arrangement

  6. I remember being taken to the zoo as a child.

    being + V3 someone took me

  7. There is a lot of work to be done.

    to be + V3 work that must be done

  8. He expects to be promoted next year.

    to be + V3 they will promote him

  9. You are not to blame. = You are not to be blamed.

    active form, passive meaning both are correct

  10. This shirt needs washing. = This shirt needs to be washed.

    need + -ing -ing after need has a passive meaning

8Questions and phrasal verbs in the passive10 examples

In a question the auxiliary comes before the subject; a phrasal verb keeps its particle.

  1. Was the window broken by the boys?

    yes / no question Did the boys break the window?

  2. Is Odia spoken in Odisha?

    yes / no question Do people speak Odia …?

  3. Has the work been completed?

    yes / no question Have they completed the work?

  4. Will the results be declared tomorrow?

    yes / no question Will they declare the results …?

  5. When was this temple built?

    wh-question When did they build this temple?

  6. Why were you punished?

    wh-question Why did the teacher punish you?

  7. By whom was this song sung?

    wh-question Who sang this song?

  8. The meeting was called off.

    phrasal verb They called off the meeting.

  9. The old tree was cut down.

    phrasal verb keep down after cut

  10. A dog was run over by a bus.

    phrasal verb A bus ran over a dog.

9When there is no passive10 examples

Intransitive and linking verbs, verbs of having and resembling, and sentences with reflexive or reciprocal objects have no passive.

  1. The baby slept for three hours.

    intransitive no object, so no passive

  2. The plane took off on time.

    intransitive phrasal verb no passive

  3. This bag costs five hundred rupees.

    linking / measure verb not * Five hundred rupees is cost …

  4. Rina resembles her mother.

    resemble not * Her mother is resembled by Rina.

  5. He has a big house.

    have (possess) not * A big house is had by him.

  6. This dress suits you.

    suit no passive

  7. She lacks confidence.

    lack no passive

  8. The boys hurt themselves.

    reflexive object not * Themselves were hurt.

  9. The two friends helped each other.

    reciprocal object not * Each other was helped.

  10. Gandhiji was born in 1869.

    passive only be born has no active form

10With or without by + agent10 examples

Use by + agent when the doer is important or new; leave it out when the doer is unknown, obvious or unimportant.

  1. My purse was stolen.

    no agent the doer is unknown

  2. The thief was arrested.

    no agent obviously by the police

  3. Rice is grown in Odisha.

    no agent by farmers — obvious

  4. English is taught in all schools.

    no agent by teachers — obvious

  5. The window was broken last night.

    no agent we do not know who

  6. Gitanjali was written by Rabindranath Tagore.

    by + agent the writer is important information

  7. The telephone was invented by Graham Bell.

    by + agent the inventor matters

  8. The village was flooded by the Mahanadi.

    by + agent the cause is important

  9. The prizes were given away by the Chief Minister.

    by + agent an important person

  10. The road was blocked by a fallen tree.

    by + agent (a thing) the cause of the event
