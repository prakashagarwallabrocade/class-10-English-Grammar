1QuestionSection A · 1 marksEasy

The finite verb in "She has been living here for years" is:

  1. (a) has ✓
  2. (b) been
  3. (c) living
  4. (d) years

Answer: (a) has

Show the explanation
  1. The first word of the verb phrase "has been living" carries the tense (has → had), so "has" is finite; "been" and "living" are non-finite.

2QuestionSection A · 1 marksEasy

"Please pass me the salt." is a/an _____ sentence.

  1. (a) declarative
  2. (b) interrogative
  3. (c) imperative ✓
  4. (d) exclamatory

Answer: (c) "Please pass me the salt." is a/an imperative sentence.

Show the explanation
  1. It is a request; it begins with the base verb "pass" and the subject "you" is understood.

3QuestionSection A · 1 marksEasy

"What a lovely garden this is!" is a/an _____ sentence.

  1. (a) exclamatory ✓
  2. (b) imperative
  3. (c) interrogative
  4. (d) declarative

Answer: (a) "What a lovely garden this is!" is a/an exclamatory sentence.

Show the explanation
  1. It begins with "What", expresses admiration and ends with "!".

4QuestionSection A · 1 marksEasy

"Did you see the match?" is a _____.

  1. (a) Wh-question
  2. (b) Yes/No question ✓
  3. (c) statement
  4. (d) command

Answer: (b) "Did you see the match?" is a Yes/No question.

Show the explanation
  1. It begins with the auxiliary "Did" and can be answered with yes or no.

5QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

Choose the exclamatory form of "It is a very tall tower."

  1. (a) How a tall tower it is!
  2. (b) What a tall tower it is! ✓
  3. (c) What tall tower it is!
  4. (d) How tall a tower is it!

Answer: (b) What a tall tower it is!

Show the explanation
  1. "a very + adjective + noun" becomes "What a + adjective + noun + subject + verb !".
  2. Option (c) drops the article "a"; (d) puts the verb before the subject, as in a question.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: D

6QuestionSection A · 1 marksEasy

Work hard, _____ you will fail.

  1. (a) and
  2. (b) or ✓
  3. (c) so
  4. (d) for

Answer: (b) Work hard, or you will fail.

Show the explanation
  1. A command followed by a warning is joined with "or" (= if you don’t).

7QuestionSection A · 1 marksEasy

He is poor, _____ he is honest.

  1. (a) but ✓
  2. (b) or
  3. (c) so
  4. (d) for

Answer: (a) He is poor, but he is honest.

Show the explanation
  1. The two clauses contrast with each other, so "but" joins them.

8QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

Choose the correct pair to fill the blanks: "She can _____ sing _____ dance."

  1. (a) either … nor
  2. (b) both … and ✓
  3. (c) neither … or
  4. (d) not only … and

Answer: (b) both … and — She can both sing and dance.

Show the explanation
  1. Correlatives go in fixed pairs: either … or, neither … nor, not only … but also, both … and. Only "both … and" is a correct pair here.

9QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

Which of these is a complex sentence?

  1. (a) He came and sat down.
  2. (b) He came early.
  3. (c) He came when I called him. ✓
  4. (d) Come here.

Answer: (c) He came when I called him.

Show the explanation
  1. It has a main clause "He came" and a subordinate clause "when I called him", which cannot stand alone.
  2. (a) is compound, (b) and (d) are simple.

10QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

The pattern of the sentence "They made him captain." is:

  1. (a) SVO
  2. (b) SVOO
  3. (c) SVC
  4. (d) SVOC ✓

Answer: (d) SVOC

Show the explanation
  1. They (S) made (V) him (O) captain (C): the complement "captain" tells us about the object "him", like "They elected Mr Das President."
← Back to the question 📖 Section: A

11QuestionSection B · 2 marksEasy

Pick out the finite verbs:
(a) We must leave now.
(b) The children were playing in the garden.

Answer: (a) must (b) were

Show the explanation
  1. The first word of each verb phrase is finite: "must" (in must leave) and "were" (in were playing).

12QuestionSection B · 2 marksEasy

Change into exclamatory sentences:
(a) The sky is very blue.
(b) She is a very kind lady.

Answer: (a) How blue the sky is ! (b) What a kind lady she is !

Show the explanation
  1. (a) very + adjective → How + adjective + subject + verb.
  2. (b) a very + adjective + noun → What a + adjective + noun + subject + verb.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: D

13QuestionSection B · 2 marksMedium

Combine each pair using either … or / neither … nor:
(a) You can stay here. You can go home.
(b) He did not phone. He did not write.

Answer: (a) You can either stay here or go home. (b) He neither phoned nor wrote.

Show the explanation
  1. (a) Two alternatives: either … or.
  2. (b) Two negatives: neither … nor; the verbs become positive past forms (phoned, wrote).

15QuestionSection B · 2 marksMedium

Mark the main clause (M.C.) and the subordinate clause (S.C.):
(a) I know the man who sells vegetables.
(b) If it rains, we will stay at home.

Answer: (a) I know the man — M.C. / who sells vegetables — S.C. (b) If it rains — S.C. / we will stay at home — M.C.

Show the explanation
  1. The main clause can stand alone; the clause beginning with "who" or "if" cannot.

16QuestionSection C · 5 marksEasy

Combine each pair into a compound sentence with the conjunction given:
(a) The bell rang. The students went out. (and)
(b) He is clever. He is lazy. (but)
(c) It was raining. We stayed indoors. (so)
(d) Hurry up. You will miss the bus. (or)
(e) She was tired. She had worked all day. (for)

Answer: (a) The bell rang and the students went out. (b) He is clever but he is lazy. (c) It was raining, so we stayed indoors. (d) Hurry up, or you will miss the bus. (e) She was tired, for she had worked all day.

Show the explanation
  1. and = one event after another; but = contrast; so = result; or = warning after a command; for = reason.
  2. One mark for each correct sentence.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: III

17QuestionSection C · 5 marksMedium

Write (S) for simple, (D) for compound and (X) for complex against each sentence, and give a reason:
(a) The old man sat under the tree.
(b) I waited for an hour, but nobody came.
(c) Tell me where you live.
(d) The book which you lent me is very interesting.
(e) Raju opened the door and the dog ran out.

Answer: (a) S (b) D (c) X (d) X (e) D

Show the explanation
  1. (a) One finite verb (sat): one clause.
  2. (b) and (e): two independent clauses joined by "but" / "and".
  3. (c) "where you live" is a subordinate clause, the object of "Tell me".
  4. (d) "which you lent me" is a subordinate (relative) clause inside the main clause.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: IV
