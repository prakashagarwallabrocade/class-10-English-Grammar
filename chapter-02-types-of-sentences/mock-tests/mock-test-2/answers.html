1QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

Which of these is NOT an imperative sentence?

  1. (a) Kindly help me.
  2. (b) Never tell lies.
  3. (c) Let’s start now.
  4. (d) You are late again. ✓

Answer: (d) You are late again.

Show the explanation
  1. It has the subject "you" and makes a statement: it is declarative. The others begin with a base verb (or Let’s / Never + verb) and give a request, advice or suggestion.

2QuestionSection A · 1 marksHard

In "The teacher may have been waiting for us", the finite verb is:

  1. (a) may ✓
  2. (b) have
  3. (c) been
  4. (d) waiting

Answer: (a) may

Show the explanation
  1. Only the first word of the verb phrase is finite. In a four-word verb phrase the modal "may" comes first; "have been waiting" is non-finite.

3QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

Choose the correct sentence.

  1. (a) Neither he or his brother came.
  2. (b) Either he nor his brother came.
  3. (c) Neither he nor his brother came. ✓
  4. (d) Both he nor his brother came.

Answer: (c) Neither he nor his brother came.

Show the explanation
  1. The correct pair is neither … nor; mixing the halves of different pairs is wrong.

4QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

The declarative form of "What a fool I have been!" is:

  1. (a) I have been a great fool. ✓
  2. (b) I have not been a fool.
  3. (c) Have I been a fool?
  4. (d) Be a fool.

Answer: (a) I have been a great fool.

Show the explanation
  1. Turn "What a + noun + subject + verb" back into "subject + verb + a very/great + noun"; the meaning must not change, so it stays positive.

5QuestionSection A · 1 marksHard

Which sentence is compound?

  1. (a) Although he was tired, he kept working.
  2. (b) He was tired, yet he kept working. ✓
  3. (c) Being tired, he kept working.
  4. (d) He kept working in spite of his tiredness.

Answer: (b) He was tired, yet he kept working.

Show the explanation
  1. Two independent clauses joined by the coordinator "yet".
  2. (a) is complex (although-clause); (c) and (d) are simple (non-finite phrase, prepositional phrase).

6QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

The pattern of "My father bought me a watch." is:

  1. (a) SVO
  2. (b) SVOO ✓
  3. (c) SVC
  4. (d) SVOC

Answer: (b) SVOO

Show the explanation
  1. My father (S) bought (V) me (indirect O) a watch (direct O).

7QuestionSection B · 3 marksHard

Turn into exclamatory sentences:
(a) It is a very sad story.
(b) The child sings very sweetly.
(c) You have grown very tall.

Answer: (a) What a sad story it is ! (b) How sweetly the child sings ! (c) How tall you have grown !

Show the explanation
  1. (a) a very + adjective + noun → What a + adjective + noun.
  2. (b) very + adverb → How + adverb; (c) very + adjective → How + adjective. The subject stays before the verb.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: D

8QuestionSection B · 3 marksMedium

Combine using the correlatives given:
(a) Ravi likes cricket. Ravi likes football. (both … and)
(b) She did not cry. She did not complain. (neither … nor)
(c) He is a singer. He is also a painter. (not only … but also)

Answer: (a) Ravi likes both cricket and football. (b) She neither cried nor complained. (c) He is not only a singer but also a painter.

Show the explanation
  1. Place each half of the pair just before the two matching words: cricket / football, cried / complained, a singer / a painter.

9QuestionSection B · 3 marksHard

Rewrite each as a complex sentence, using the word in brackets:
(a) Hardworking students succeed. (who)
(b) Tell me your address. (where)
(c) I am sure of his honesty. (that)

Answer: (a) Students who work hard succeed. (b) Tell me where you live. (c) I am sure that he is honest.

Show the explanation
  1. Each phrase becomes a finite subordinate clause with its own subject and verb, as in the chapter’s pairs (Tell me the place of your birth → Tell me where you were born).

10QuestionSection B · 3 marksHard

Pick out the subordinate clause and say whether it is a noun, adverb or relative clause:
(a) What he said surprised everyone.
(b) We left before the rain started.
(c) This is the house that Jack built.

Answer: (a) What he said — noun clause (b) before the rain started — adverb clause (c) that Jack built — relative clause

Show the explanation
  1. (a) The clause is the subject of "surprised": it works as a noun.
  2. (b) It tells when we left: an adverb clause of time.
  3. (c) It describes "the house": a relative (adjective) clause.

11QuestionSection C · 4 marksHard

Do as directed:
(a) Open the window. (Make it negative.)
(b) She has finished her work. (Change into a Yes/No question.)
(c) It is a very cold morning. (Change into an exclamatory sentence.)
(d) He lives in Puri. (Ask a Wh-question about the underlined words.)

Answer: (a) Don’t open the window. (b) Has she finished her work? (c) What a cold morning it is ! (d) Where does he live?

Show the explanation
  1. (a) Imperatives are made negative with Don’t + base verb.
  2. (b) Move the auxiliary "has" before the subject.
  3. (c) a very + adjective + noun → What a + adjective + noun + subject + verb.
  4. (d) A place is asked about with "Where"; add does and use the base verb "live".
← Back to the question 📖 Section: II

12QuestionSection C · 4 marksMedium

Write five sentences about your school, using at least one declarative, one interrogative, one imperative and one exclamatory sentence. Name the kind of each sentence.

Answer: Sample: My school stands on the bank of a river. (declarative) Have you ever seen our school garden? (interrogative) Come and see our new library. (imperative) What a beautiful library it is! (exclamatory) Our teachers are very kind. (declarative)

Show the explanation
  1. A declarative sentence states something; an interrogative one asks; an imperative one begins with the base verb; an exclamatory one begins with What or How and ends with "!".
  2. Marks: one for each kind used correctly and named.

13QuestionSection C · 4 marksHard

Write (S), (D) or (X) against each sentence and give a reason:
(a) Rita came, saw the mess and started cleaning.
(b) The man whom you met yesterday is my uncle.
(c) In spite of the rain, the match continued.
(d) He failed because he did not work hard.

Answer: (a) D (b) X (c) S (d) X

Show the explanation
  1. (a) Three independent clauses joined by "and" (Rita came / (she) saw the mess / (she) started cleaning).
  2. (b) "whom you met yesterday" is a relative clause: complex.
  3. (c) Only one finite verb (continued); "In spite of the rain" is a phrase, not a clause.
  4. (d) "because he did not work hard" is an adverb clause of reason: complex.
