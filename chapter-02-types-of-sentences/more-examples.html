1Finite verbs and simple sentences10 examples

The finite verb is the first word of the verb phrase. A simple sentence has one finite clause.

  1. My sister sings in the school choir.

    finite: sings a one-word verb phrase is itself the finite verb

  2. The train has left the platform.

    finite: has first word of the verb phrase has left

  3. They were playing football in the rain.

    finite: were first word of were playing

  4. You must wear a helmet on a scooter.

    finite: must a modal is the finite verb

  5. The new bridge will be opened next month.

    finite: will first word of will be opened

  6. She has been teaching here since 2010.

    finite: has has been teaching — only has changes for tense (had)

  7. My uncle did not come to the wedding.

    finite: did in a negative, the auxiliary do / did is finite

  8. Can you solve this sum?

    finite: can in a question the finite verb comes before the subject

  9. Rabi wants to become a doctor.

    finite: wants to become is non-finite; the sentence still has one finite clause

  10. The old man sat under the banyan tree, watching the children.

    simple sentence one finite verb (sat); watching is non-finite

2Declarative sentences10 examples

A declarative sentence makes a statement. Its five basic patterns are SV, SVO, SVOO, SVC and SVOC.

  1. The baby | smiled.

    SV an intransitive verb needs no object

  2. The sun | rose | early.

    SV (+ adverb) early is an adverb, not an object

  3. Mother | cooked | rice.

    SVO rice is the object of cooked

  4. The students | planted | twenty trees in the school garden.

    SVO a transitive verb with its object

  5. My uncle | sent | me | a birthday card.

    SVOO me = indirect object, a birthday card = direct object

  6. The teacher | asked | us | a difficult question.

    SVOO two objects after asked

  7. Sita | became | a nurse.

    SVC a nurse completes the subject

  8. The milk | tastes | sour.

    SVC sour describes the subject after a linking verb

  9. The people | made | him | their leader.

    SVOC their leader describes the object him

  10. The news | made | everyone | happy.

    SVOC happy completes the object

3Interrogative sentences10 examples

Yes / No questions begin with an auxiliary; Wh-questions begin with a question word.

  1. Do you speak Odia at home?

    Yes / No question do + subject + V1

  2. Is your father a farmer?

    Yes / No question be comes before the subject

  3. Have you finished your project?

    Yes / No question has / have + subject + V3

  4. Can I borrow your pen for a minute?

    Yes / No question a modal comes first

  5. Did the bus arrive on time?

    Yes / No question did + subject + V1 (not arrived)

  6. Where do you live?

    Wh-question asks for a place

  7. Why were you absent yesterday?

    Wh-question asks for a reason

  8. How many brothers have you got?

    Wh-question asks for a number

  9. Who broke the window?

    Wh-question (subject) who is the subject, so no do / did

  10. Which colour do you like best?

    Wh-question a choice from a known set

4Imperative sentences10 examples

The subject you is left out and the sentence begins with the base form of the verb.

  1. Close the windows before you leave.

    order begins with the base form close

  2. Please lend me your dictionary.

    request please makes it polite

  3. Drink plenty of water in summer.

    advice base form drink

  4. Don’t touch the wires.

    warning negative imperative — do not + V1

  5. Let’s visit Chilika Lake this Sunday.

    suggestion let’s + V1 includes the speaker

  6. Stand in a straight line, everybody.

    command a teacher or leader giving a command

  7. Add two spoons of sugar and stir well.

    instructions steps of a recipe or process

  8. Forgive us, Lord.

    prayer an imperative addressed to God

  9. Have a happy birthday!

    wish have + noun for good wishes

  10. Never tell lies.

    advice (negative) never + V1 is a strong negative imperative

5Exclamatory sentences10 examples

An exclamatory sentence shows a strong feeling. It begins with What or How and ends with “!”.

  1. What a beautiful garden this is!

    What a + adjective + noun admiration — a singular countable noun

  2. What lovely flowers these are!

    What + adjective + plural noun no a / an before a plural noun

  3. What terrible weather we are having!

    What + adjective + uncountable noun weather takes no a

  4. How tall this tree is!

    How + adjective subject + verb come after the adjective

  5. How fast the horse runs!

    How + adverb fast describes runs

  6. What an honest boy he is!

    What an + adjective + noun an before a vowel sound (honest)

  7. How kind of you to help me!

    How + adjective gratitude

  8. What a pity you missed the show!

    What a + noun sorrow or regret

  9. She is a very clever girl. → What a clever girl she is!

    declarative → exclamatory very + adjective + noun becomes What a + adjective + noun

  10. The river is very wide. → How wide the river is!

    declarative → exclamatory very + adjective becomes How + adjective

6Compound sentences10 examples

Two or more independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction (and, but, or, so …) or a pair of correlatives.

  1. I knocked at the door, and my friend opened it.

    and adds one action to another

  2. The shop was open, but there was nobody inside.

    but a contrast

  3. You can pay now or you can pay later.

    or a choice

  4. It was raining heavily, so the match was cancelled.

    so a result

  5. He is poor, yet he never complains.

    yet an unexpected contrast

  6. Hurry up, or else you will miss the bus.

    or else a warning — if not … then …

  7. She must be at home, for her bicycle is outside.

    for gives the reason (formal)

  8. Either you tidy your room or you stay at home.

    either … or correlatives join two alternatives

  9. He neither wrote to me nor phoned.

    neither … nor a negative pair

  10. She not only cleaned the house but also cooked dinner.

    not only … but also adds a second, stronger point

7Complex sentences10 examples

A main clause with one or more subordinate clauses — noun, adverb or relative (adjective) clauses.

  1. I know that he is honest.

    noun clause object of know

  2. What she said surprised everyone.

    noun clause subject of surprised

  3. Tell me where you bought this shirt.

    noun clause object of tell

  4. I wonder whether it will rain today.

    noun clause an indirect yes / no question

  5. The girl who won the race is my cousin.

    relative clause describes the girl

  6. This is the house where Gopabandhu Das was born.

    relative clause describes the house

  7. We stayed indoors because it was very hot.

    adverb clause (reason) answers why?

  8. When the bell rang, the students ran out.

    adverb clause (time) answers when?

  9. If you work hard, you will pass.

    adverb clause (condition) a condition for the main clause

  10. I am sure of winning the match. → I am sure that I will win the match.

    simple → complex a non-finite phrase becomes a finite noun clause
