1Main, coordinate and subordinate clauses10 examples

A main clause can stand alone; a coordinate clause is joined to it by and, but, or, so; a subordinate clause cannot stand alone.

  1. The bell rang and the students went home.

    main clause can stand alone

  2. Mohan finished his homework and his sister helped their mother.

    coordinate clause joined by and; also independent

  3. I wanted to go out, but it was raining.

    main clause first independent clause

  4. The shop was open, but the shopkeeper was asleep.

    coordinate clause joined by but

  5. You can phone me or you can send a message.

    coordinate clause joined by or

  6. She stayed in bed because she had a fever.

    subordinate clause (adverb) cannot stand alone

  7. I know that you are busy.

    subordinate clause (noun) object of know

  8. The man who lives next door is a pilot.

    subordinate clause (relative) describes the man

  9. After finishing his work, he went to bed.

    non-finite subordinate clause finishing has no tense

  10. He went to bed after he had finished his work.

    finite subordinate clause had carries the tense

2Noun clauses: subject and object10 examples

A noun clause does the work of a noun. It usually begins with that, if / whether or a wh-word.

  1. What you said made me happy.

    subject what made me happy?

  2. That the earth is round is a known fact.

    subject that-clause as subject

  3. Whether we go or not depends on the weather.

    subject whether-clause as subject

  4. Who will be the captain has not been decided.

    subject wh-clause as subject

  5. I think (that) he is right.

    object that can be left out

  6. She asked me where I lived.

    object the second object of asked

  7. Do you know when the train will arrive?

    object statement word order after when

  8. I wonder if he has got my letter.

    object if = whether

  9. Nobody knows how the fire started.

    object object of knows

  10. Tell me what you want.

    object tell me what?

3Noun clauses: complement, after a preposition, in apposition10 examples

A noun clause can complete a linking verb, follow a preposition, or explain the noun before it.

  1. The truth is that I forgot your birthday.

    complement completes is

  2. My hope is that you will win.

    complement completes is

  3. The question was how we would cross the river.

    complement completes was

  4. This is where we first met.

    complement completes is

  5. Pay attention to what the teacher says.

    object of a preposition after to

  6. She was surprised at how quickly he finished.

    object of a preposition after at

  7. We talked about where we should go for the holidays.

    object of a preposition after about

  8. The news that our team had won spread quickly.

    apposition explains the news

  9. The fact that he lied upset his parents.

    apposition explains the fact

  10. It is certain that she will come.

    apposition to it = That she will come is certain.

4Relative clauses: who, whom, whose, which, that10 examples

A relative clause describes the noun before it. who / whom for people, which for things, that for both, whose for possession.

  1. The girl who sings in the choir is my sister.

    who a person, subject of sings

  2. The man whom we met at the station was very helpful.

    whom a person, object of met

  3. That is the boy whose bicycle was stolen.

    whose possession (his bicycle)

  4. I lost the pen which my uncle gave me.

    which a thing

  5. The film that we saw last night was exciting.

    that a thing

  6. Here is the book you asked for.

    relative pronoun left out = which / that you asked for (object)

  7. This is the village where my grandparents live.

    where a place

  8. I remember the day when we first came to Bhubaneswar.

    when a time

  9. The dog that bit the child has been caught.

    that an animal, subject of bit

  10. Do you know the reason why she left?

    why after the reason

5Defining and non-defining relative clauses10 examples

A defining clause tells us which one we mean (no commas). A non-defining clause only adds information (commas, no that).

  1. The students who passed the test got certificates.

    defining only those students

  2. The students, who all passed the test, got certificates.

    non-defining all the students passed

  3. The house that stands on the corner is for sale.

    defining tells us which house

  4. Rabindranath Tagore, who wrote Gitanjali, won the Nobel Prize.

    non-defining a name is already clear; the clause only adds

  5. My father, who is a doctor, works in Cuttack.

    non-defining I have only one father

  6. The doctor who treated me was very kind.

    defining which doctor

  7. Puri, which is famous for its temple, is on the coast.

    non-defining commas; which, not that

  8. The bag I bought yesterday is already torn.

    defining object pronoun left out

  9. My bag, which I bought yesterday, is already torn.

    non-defining which cannot be left out here

  10. Mr Das, whose son is my classmate, is our new neighbour.

    non-defining whose in a non-defining clause

6Joining sentences with a relative pronoun10 examples

Find the word common to both sentences and replace it with who / which / whose / where in the second.

  1. I met a girl. She speaks four languages. → I met a girl who speaks four languages.

    who she → who

  2. This is the pen. I bought it yesterday. → This is the pen (which) I bought yesterday.

    which it → which (can be dropped)

  3. The man is a farmer. His cow won a prize. → The man whose cow won a prize is a farmer.

    whose his → whose

  4. We visited a town. Gandhiji stayed there. → We visited the town where Gandhiji stayed.

    where there → where

  5. The boy is absent. I gave him a book. → The boy (whom) I gave a book to is absent.

    whom him → whom (can be dropped)

  6. Konark is in Odisha. It has a Sun Temple. → Konark, which has a Sun Temple, is in Odisha.

    which (non-defining) a place name needs commas

  7. I know a shop. It sells old books. → I know a shop that sells old books.

    that it → that

  8. The lady is my aunt. She is wearing a red sari. → The lady who is wearing a red sari is my aunt.

    who she → who

  9. I still remember the year. We moved then. → I still remember the year when we moved.

    when then → when

  10. The teacher praised the student. His answers were correct. → The teacher praised the student whose answers were correct.

    whose his → whose
