1Question

In the following four columns some proverbs or quotations have been given. Expressions in column A and column D belong to the same proverb. Put a suitable relative pronoun in column B and choose the correct expression from column C. In this way write the proverbs. As soon as you complete, raise your hand and draw the teacher’s attention.

ABCD
All———pays the piperis not gold.
People———laughs lastshould not throw stones.
All’s well———prays togetherwell.
He————glitterscalls the tune.
He———blows nobodylaughs best.
It’s a long lane———do not want——— .
It’s an ill wind———endsany good.
There are no one so deaf as those——live in glass housesto hear.
The family——has no turningstays together.

Rules used

  • Relative clauses

    A relative (adjective) clause describes the noun before it and begins with who, whom, which, that, whose (or where for a place). To combine two sentences, put the relative pronoun after the common noun.

    📖 Relative Clauses
Note: The usual form of proverb 8 is 'There are none so deaf as those who will not hear'; the book prints 'no one'.
  1. Match each beginning (column A) with its ending (column D), then choose the middle part from column C so that the proverb makes sense.
  2. Use 'who' after people and 'he', and 'that' after 'all', 'it’s a … that' and things.

Answer: 1. All that glitters is not gold.
2. People who live in glass houses should not throw stones.
3. All’s well that ends well.
4. He who pays the piper calls the tune.
5. He who laughs last laughs best.
6. It’s a long lane that has no turning.
7. It’s an ill wind that blows nobody any good.
8. There are no one so deaf as those who do not want to hear.
9. The family that prays together stays together.
