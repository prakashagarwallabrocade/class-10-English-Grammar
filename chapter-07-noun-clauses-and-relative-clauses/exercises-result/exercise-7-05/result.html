1Question

The birds are chirping. + The birds live in the neighbourhood.

Rules used

  • Relative clauses

    A relative (adjective) clause describes the noun before it and begins with who, whom, which, that, whose (or where for a place). To combine two sentences, put the relative pronoun after the common noun.

    📖 Relative Clauses
  1. Common word 'birds'; the second sentence becomes the which-clause.

Answer: The birds which live in the neighbourhood are chirping.

2Question

I write with the pen. + My father bought the pen for me.

Rules used (as above)
  • Relative clauses

    A relative (adjective) clause describes the noun before it and begins with who, whom, which, that, whose (or where for a place). To combine two sentences, put the relative pronoun after the common noun.

    📖 Relative Clauses
  1. Common word 'pen'; 'which' is the object, so 'the pen my father bought for me' is also correct.

Answer: I write with the pen which my father bought for me.

7Question

The chair has a cracked leg. + He is sitting in the chair.

Rules used (as above)
  • Relative clauses

    A relative (adjective) clause describes the noun before it and begins with who, whom, which, that, whose (or where for a place). To combine two sentences, put the relative pronoun after the common noun.

    📖 Relative Clauses
  1. 'in the chair' becomes 'in which' (or: the chair he is sitting in).

Answer: The chair in which he is sitting has a cracked leg.
