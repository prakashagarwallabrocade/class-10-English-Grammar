1QuestionSection A · 1 marksEasy

"in the morning" is a:

  1. (a) phrase ✓
  2. (b) clause
  3. (c) sentence
  4. (d) main clause

Answer: (a) phrase

Show the explanation
  1. It has no subject and no finite verb, so it is a phrase.

2QuestionSection A · 1 marksEasy

The book _____ you gave me is interesting.

  1. (a) which ✓
  2. (b) who
  3. (c) whose
  4. (d) where

Answer: (a) The book which you gave me is interesting.

Show the explanation
  1. A thing (the book), object of "gave": which (or that, or no pronoun at all).

3QuestionSection A · 1 marksEasy

She is the girl _____ won the prize.

  1. (a) who ✓
  2. (b) which
  3. (c) whom
  4. (d) whose

Answer: (a) She is the girl who won the prize.

Show the explanation
  1. A person who is the subject of "won": who.

4QuestionSection A · 1 marksEasy

I know _____ he lives.

  1. (a) where ✓
  2. (b) which
  3. (c) whose
  4. (d) whom

Answer: (a) I know where he lives.

Show the explanation
  1. The noun clause "where he lives" is the object of "know"; it asks about a place.

5QuestionSection A · 1 marksEasy

This is the boy _____ father is a doctor.

  1. (a) whose ✓
  2. (b) who
  3. (c) whom
  4. (d) which

Answer: (a) This is the boy whose father is a doctor.

6QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

In "What she said is true", the noun clause works as the:

  1. (a) subject ✓
  2. (b) object
  3. (c) complement
  4. (d) object of a preposition

Answer: (a) subject

Show the explanation
  1. What is true? — "What she said": the subject of "is".

7QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

In "I hope that he will come", the clause "that he will come" works as the:

  1. (a) subject
  2. (b) object ✓
  3. (c) complement
  4. (d) object of a preposition

Answer: (b) object

Show the explanation
  1. I hope what? — the that-clause is the object of the transitive verb "hope".

8QuestionSection A · 1 marksEasy

The house _____ I was born is near the river.

  1. (a) where ✓
  2. (b) which
  3. (c) who
  4. (d) whose

Answer: (a) The house where I was born is near the river.

Show the explanation
  1. A place (= in which): where. "which" would need "in" (the house in which I was born).

9QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

Which sentence has a non-defining relative clause?

  1. (a) The man who came yesterday is my uncle.
  2. (b) My father, who is a doctor, lives in Delhi. ✓
  3. (c) The book that I bought is new.
  4. (d) I know a girl who sings well.

Answer: (b) My father, who is a doctor, lives in Delhi.

Show the explanation
  1. The clause gives extra information about a person already known (my father) and is set off by commas.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: Relative Clauses

10QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

In which sentence can the relative pronoun be dropped?

  1. (a) The boy who won is my cousin.
  2. (b) The pen that I lost was red. ✓
  3. (c) Rita, who sings well, is here.
  4. (d) The dog which barked ran away.

Answer: (b) The pen that I lost was red.

Show the explanation
  1. "that" is the object of "lost" in a defining clause: The pen I lost was red.
  2. In the others the pronoun is the subject, or the clause is non-defining.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: Relative Clauses

11QuestionSection B · 2 marksEasy

Combine using who or which:
(a) I met a man. He was blind.
(b) This is the bag. I bought it yesterday.

Answer: (a) I met a man who was blind. (b) This is the bag which I bought yesterday.

Show the explanation
  1. Put the relative pronoun after the common noun (man, bag) and drop the repeated pronoun (he, it).

12QuestionSection B · 2 marksEasy

Say whether each is a phrase or a clause:
(a) because it was raining
(b) because of the rain

Answer: (a) clause (b) phrase

Show the explanation
  1. (a) has a subject and a finite verb (it was raining); (b) has none.

13QuestionSection B · 2 marksMedium

Pick out the noun clause and say how it works:
(a) I know that he is honest.
(b) The fact is that he is lazy.

Answer: (a) that he is honest — object (b) that he is lazy — complement

Show the explanation
  1. (a) object of "know". (b) It completes "is".

14QuestionSection B · 2 marksEasy

Fill in the blanks with relative pronouns:
(a) The man _____ car was stolen went to the police.
(b) The pen _____ you gave me writes well.

Answer: (a) The man whose car was stolen went to the police. (b) The pen which (that) you gave me writes well.

Show the explanation
  1. (a) possession: whose. (b) a thing, object: which / that.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: Relative Clauses

15QuestionSection B · 2 marksMedium

Put commas where they are necessary:
(a) My mother who is a nurse works at night.
(b) The boy who broke the window ran away.

Answer: (a) My mother, who is a nurse, works at night. (b) No commas: The boy who broke the window ran away.

Show the explanation
  1. (a) One mother: the clause only adds information (non-defining).
  2. (b) The clause tells which boy (defining).

16QuestionSection C · 5 marksEasy

Combine each pair using a relative pronoun or "where":
(a) This is the house. Jack built it.
(b) I have a friend. His father is a pilot.
(c) The girl is my sister. She is singing.
(d) Here is the village. I was born there.
(e) The letter came today. I was waiting for it.

Answer: (a) This is the house which (that) Jack built. (b) I have a friend whose father is a pilot. (c) The girl who is singing is my sister. (d) Here is the village where I was born. (e) The letter which (that) I was waiting for came today.

Show the explanation
  1. which / that for things, who for people, whose for possession, where for a place.
  2. In (c) and (e) the relative clause goes right after the noun it describes, inside the main clause.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: Relative Clauses

17QuestionSection C · 5 marksMedium

Pick out the noun clause in each sentence and say how it works:
(a) What you say is right.
(b) I don’t know where he lives.
(c) My hope is that you will pass.
(d) Listen to what the teacher says.
(e) The news that he had won surprised us.

Answer: (a) What you say — subject (b) where he lives — object (c) that you will pass — complement (d) what the teacher says — object of a preposition (e) that he had won — in apposition to "the news"

Show the explanation
  1. Ask what / whom about the verb, or see which word the clause completes or explains.
