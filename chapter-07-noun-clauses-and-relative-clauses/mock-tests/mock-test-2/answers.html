1QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

In "It is certain that he will pass", the clause "that he will pass" is:

  1. (a) the subject
  2. (b) the object
  3. (c) in apposition to "it" ✓
  4. (d) a relative clause

Answer: (c) in apposition to "it"

Show the explanation
  1. "It" stands for the that-clause, which comes after the complement (like "It is true that his father has come").

2QuestionSection A · 1 marksHard

In which sentence can "that" NOT fill the blank?

  1. (a) The book ___ I read was good.
  2. (b) Delhi, ___ is our capital, is crowded. ✓
  3. (c) The man ___ came here is my uncle.
  4. (d) All ___ glitters is not gold.

Answer: (b) Delhi, ___ is our capital, is crowded.

Show the explanation
  1. A non-defining clause (with commas) cannot take "that"; it needs "which".

3QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

In "Satish scored a goal in the last minute, which was fortunate", "which" refers to:

  1. (a) Satish
  2. (b) a goal
  3. (c) the whole main clause ✓
  4. (d) the last minute

Answer: (c) the whole main clause

Show the explanation
  1. It was fortunate that Satish scored — the non-defining clause qualifies the whole main clause.

4QuestionSection A · 1 marksHard

Choose the correct sentence.

  1. (a) The man whom I met him was kind.
  2. (b) The man whom I met was kind. ✓
  3. (c) The man who I met him was kind.
  4. (d) The man which I met was kind.

Answer: (b) The man whom I met was kind.

Show the explanation
  1. The relative pronoun replaces the object "him", so "him" must not be repeated; "which" is not used for people.

5QuestionSection A · 1 marksHard

In "He posted the letter after he had sealed it", the clause "after he had sealed it" is a:

  1. (a) finite subordinate clause ✓
  2. (b) non-finite clause
  3. (c) main clause
  4. (d) coordinate clause

Answer: (a) finite subordinate clause

Show the explanation
  1. "had" carries the past tense, so the clause is finite; it begins with "after" and cannot stand alone, so it is subordinate (compare the non-finite "after sealing the letter").

6QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

In "Whether he will come is uncertain", the noun clause works as the:

  1. (a) subject ✓
  2. (b) object
  3. (c) complement
  4. (d) object of a preposition

Answer: (a) subject

Show the explanation
  1. What is uncertain? — "Whether he will come": the subject.

7QuestionSection B · 3 marksHard

Explain the difference in meaning:
(a) The students who had finished their work went home.
(b) The students, who had finished their work, went home.

Answer: (a) Defining: only those students who had finished went home; others stayed. (b) Non-defining: all the students went home, and all of them had finished their work.

Show the explanation
  1. Without commas the clause picks out some of the students; with commas it adds information about all of them.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: Relative Clauses

8QuestionSection B · 3 marksMedium

Complete each sentence with a noun clause doing the job given in brackets:
(a) _____ is a mystery. (subject)
(b) I wonder _____ . (object)
(c) The question is _____ . (complement)

Answer: Sample: (a) How the thief escaped is a mystery. (b) I wonder whether it will rain today. (c) The question is who will bell the cat.

Show the explanation
  1. Each answer is a clause with its own subject and finite verb, beginning with a wh-word, whether / if or that.

9QuestionSection B · 3 marksHard

Combine each pair, using a preposition + which / whom:
(a) This is the chair. He was sitting on it.
(b) The girl is my friend. I was talking to her.
(c) The company is very large. My father works for it.

Answer: (a) This is the chair on which he was sitting. (b) The girl to whom I was talking is my friend. (c) The company for which my father works is very large.

Show the explanation
  1. The preposition moves in front of the relative pronoun: on it → on which, to her → to whom, for it → for which.

10QuestionSection B · 3 marksHard

Drop the relative pronoun where possible; otherwise write "not possible" and say why:
(a) The film that we saw was boring.
(b) The man who lives next door is a pilot.
(c) Rita, whom you met yesterday, is my cousin.

Answer: (a) The film we saw was boring. (b) Not possible — "who" is the subject. (c) Not possible — it is a non-defining clause.

Show the explanation
  1. A relative pronoun can be dropped only when it is the object in a defining clause.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: Relative Clauses

11QuestionSection C · 4 marksHard

Find the four subordinate clauses in this passage and say whether each is a noun clause or a relative clause:
I don’t know why Raju is absent. The boy who sits next to him says that he is ill. The doctor whom his parents called came yesterday.

Answer: why Raju is absent — noun clause (object of "know"); who sits next to him — relative clause (describes "the boy"); that he is ill — noun clause (object of "says"); whom his parents called — relative clause (describes "the doctor")

Show the explanation
  1. A noun clause does the work of a noun; a relative clause comes after a noun and describes it.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: Noun Clauses

12QuestionSection C · 4 marksMedium

Define each word with a relative clause: (a) a carpenter (b) a library (c) a thermometer (d) a dentist.

Answer: Sample: (a) A carpenter is a person who makes things out of wood. (b) A library is a place where books are kept for people to read. (c) A thermometer is an instrument which measures temperature. (d) A dentist is a doctor who treats people’s teeth.

Show the explanation
  1. who for people, which for things, where for places. One mark for each definition.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: Relative Clauses

13QuestionSection C · 4 marksHard

Correct the errors:
(a) This is the boy which broke the glass.
(b) My brother, that lives in Pune, is an engineer.
(c) The house where I live in is old.
(d) I know the girl whose her father is a doctor.

Answer: (a) This is the boy who broke the glass. (b) My brother, who lives in Pune, is an engineer. (c) The house where I live is old. (d) I know the girl whose father is a doctor.

Show the explanation
  1. (a) "who" for people. (b) A non-defining clause cannot use "that".
  2. (c) "where" already means "in which", so "in" is extra. (d) "whose" replaces "her"; do not use both.
