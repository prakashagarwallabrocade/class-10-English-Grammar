1Ways of expressing the present time10 examples

The present simple for habits, facts and states; the present progressive for what is going on now or these days.

  1. Water boils at 100 °C.

    Present simple a scientific truth

  2. My grandfather reads the newspaper every morning.

    Present simple a habit (every morning)

  3. The Mahanadi flows into the Bay of Bengal.

    Present simple a permanent fact

  4. Rina doesn’t like spicy food.

    Present simple (negative) a state verb (like) stays simple

  5. Does this bag belong to you?

    Present simple (question) belong is a verb of state

  6. Look! The children are flying kites on the roof.

    Present progressive happening right now (Look!)

  7. Please be quiet. The baby is sleeping.

    Present progressive going on at the moment of speaking

  8. We are learning French these days.

    Present progressive an activity over a period of time (these days)

  9. My brother is staying with us this week while his house is being painted.

    Present progressive a temporary situation

  10. Why are you laughing?

    Present progressive (question) asking about an action going on now

2Ways of expressing the past time10 examples

The past simple and past progressive for finished past time; the present perfect and the past perfect link one time to another.

  1. Gandhiji visited Cuttack in 1921.

    Past simple a finished action at a stated past time

  2. She opened the door, switched on the light and sat down.

    Past simple one event after another in a story

  3. I was having dinner when the phone rang.

    Past progressive + past simple an action in progress interrupted by a shorter one

  4. At eight o’clock last night we were watching a cricket match.

    Past progressive in progress at a past moment

  5. I have just finished my homework, so I can come out now.

    Present perfect a past action with a result now (just)

  6. Have you ever seen the Sun Temple at Konark?

    Present perfect (question) experience up to now (ever), no time given

  7. Sorry, I’m out of breath. I have been running.

    Present perfect progressive the effect of a recent activity shows now

  8. They have been living in Bhubaneswar since 2015.

    Present perfect progressive began in the past and still going on (since)

  9. When we reached the cinema, the film had already started.

    Past perfect the earlier of two past actions

  10. The farmers had been waiting for rain for weeks before the monsoon finally arrived.

    Past perfect progressive going on for some time before another past action

3Ways of expressing the future time10 examples

will, shall and be going to, plus the present tenses and the future progressive / perfect.

  1. The phone is ringing. I will answer it.

    will + V1 a decision made at the moment of speaking

  2. Don’t worry, I will never forget your help.

    will + V1 a promise

  3. Shall I carry that heavy box for you?

    Shall I + V1 an offer of help

  4. I am going to buy a new bicycle with my prize money.

    be going to + V1 an intention decided earlier

  5. Look at those dark clouds. It is going to rain.

    be going to + V1 something we can already see coming

  6. My cousins are arriving from Delhi tomorrow morning.

    Present progressive a plan already arranged

  7. The Puri Express leaves at 9.15 pm.

    Present simple a timetable or official programme

  8. I will phone you as soon as I reach home.

    Present simple in a time clause after as soon as / when / before, no will

  9. This time next week we will be sitting for our board examination.

    will be + V-ing in progress at a future time

  10. By the end of March, the workers will have completed the new bridge.

    will have + V3 completed by a future time
