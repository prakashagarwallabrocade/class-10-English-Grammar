1QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

By the time we reach the cinema, the film _____.

  1. (a) will start
  2. (b) will have started ✓
  3. (c) starts
  4. (d) has started

Answer: (b) By the time we reach the cinema, the film will have started.

Show the explanation
  1. "By the time …" points to an action completed before a future moment: will have + V3.

2QuestionSection A · 1 marksHard

How long _____ English when you moved to Delhi?

  1. (a) did you study
  2. (b) have you been studying
  3. (c) had you been studying ✓
  4. (d) were you studying

Answer: (c) How long had you been studying English when you moved to Delhi?

Show the explanation
  1. "How long … when you moved" asks about an activity going on up to a past moment: past perfect progressive (had been + V-ing).
  2. "have you been studying" would reach up to now, not up to the move.

3QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

In which sentence does the present progressive refer to a future arrangement?

  1. (a) I am reading a novel now.
  2. (b) We are flying to Delhi next Monday. ✓
  3. (c) The river is rising fast.
  4. (d) She is staying with her aunt at the moment.

Answer: (b) We are flying to Delhi next Monday.

Show the explanation
  1. Only this sentence has a future time ("next Monday") for a planned journey; the others describe what is happening now or around now.

4QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

Choose the correct sentence.

  1. (a) When have you finished the work?
  2. (b) When did you finish the work? ✓
  3. (c) I have finished it yesterday.
  4. (d) I finished it since morning.

Answer: (b) When did you finish the work?

Show the explanation
  1. A question with "When" asks for a past time, so it takes the past simple.
  2. "have finished … yesterday" mixes the present perfect with a finished time; "since" needs the perfect.

5QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

This time tomorrow I _____ in the train to Puri.

  1. (a) will travel
  2. (b) will have travelled
  3. (c) will be travelling ✓
  4. (d) travel

Answer: (c) This time tomorrow I will be travelling in the train to Puri.

Show the explanation
  1. "This time tomorrow" is a future moment when the action will be in progress: will be + V-ing.

6QuestionSection A · 1 marksHard

I _____ this book for two hours, but I haven’t finished it yet.

  1. (a) have read
  2. (b) have been reading ✓
  3. (c) read
  4. (d) had read

Answer: (b) I have been reading this book for two hours, but I haven’t finished it yet.

Show the explanation
  1. The reading started two hours ago and is still going on (not finished yet): present perfect progressive.
  2. "have read" would mean the reading is complete, which contradicts "haven’t finished".

7QuestionSection B · 3 marksHard

Join each pair into one sentence, beginning with the word in brackets:
(a) I locked the door. Then I went to bed. (After)
(b) The guests left. We reached the party late. (By the time)
(c) He saw the snake. Immediately he screamed. (As soon as)

Answer: (a) After I had locked the door, I went to bed. (b) By the time we reached the party, the guests had left. (c) As soon as he saw the snake, he screamed.

Show the explanation
  1. (a) Locking the door came first: past perfect after "After", past simple in the main clause.
  2. (b) The guests left before we arrived; "By the time" + past simple, and the earlier action in the past perfect.
  3. (c) The two actions follow each other immediately; both can be in the past simple. (As soon as he had seen the snake … is also correct.)
← Back to the question 📖 Section: Ways of expressing the past time

8QuestionSection B · 3 marksMedium

Find the error in each sentence and correct it:
(a) Raju is having a new car.
(b) I didn’t saw him at the market.
(c) She has gone to Delhi last week.

Answer: (a) Raju has a new car. (b) I didn’t see him at the market. (c) She went to Delhi last week.

Show the explanation
  1. (a) "have" meaning "own" is a verb of state: present simple.
  2. (b) After did / didn’t the main verb is V1.
  3. (c) "last week" is a finished time: past simple, not present perfect.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: Ways of expressing the present time

9QuestionSection B · 3 marksHard

Complete each sentence with will be V-ing or will have V-en (V3) of the verb in brackets:
(a) Don’t phone at eight tonight; we _____ (have) dinner then.
(b) By December, the workers _____ (build) the new bridge.
(c) When your train arrives, I _____ (wait) for you at the gate.

Answer: (a) we will be having dinner (b) the workers will have built the new bridge (c) I will be waiting for you

Show the explanation
  1. (a) and (c): an action in progress at a future moment → will be + V-ing.
  2. (b) "By December": completed before a future time → will have + V3 (build → built).

10QuestionSection B · 3 marksHard

Explain the difference in meaning between the sentences in each pair:
(a) I have done my homework. / I have been doing my homework.
(b) I’ll take a taxi. / I’m going to take a taxi.

Answer: (a) The first says the homework is finished; the second says I spent time on it and it may not be finished. (b) The first is a decision made at the moment of speaking; the second is a plan made before speaking.

Show the explanation
  1. Present perfect simple = completed result; present perfect progressive = the activity over a period (its effect, not its completion).
  2. "will" = decision now / offer; "be going to" = intention already decided.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: Ways of expressing the future time

11QuestionSection C · 4 marksHard

Fill in the blanks with suitable forms of the verbs in brackets (½ mark each):
Ramesh _____ (work) in this office since 2010. Last year he _____ (get) a promotion. When I _____ (meet) him yesterday, he _____ (look) very tired, because he _____ (work) late every night that week. He told me that he _____ (already / apply) for leave. Next month he _____ (go) to Shimla with his family; they _____ (book) the tickets already.

Answer: Ramesh has been working in this office since 2010. Last year he got a promotion. When I met him yesterday, he looked very tired, because he had been working late every night that week. He told me that he had already applied for leave. Next month he is going to Shimla with his family; they have booked the tickets already.

Show the explanation
  1. "since 2010" up to now: present perfect progressive (has worked is also accepted).
  2. "Last year", "yesterday": past simple — got, met, looked.
  3. His tiredness yesterday was the effect of an activity before it: past perfect progressive "had been working". The application came before he told me: past perfect "had already applied".
  4. A fixed plan for next month: present progressive "is going" (or "is going to go"). Tickets bought with a present result: present perfect "have booked".
← Back to the question 📖 Section: Ways of expressing the past time

12QuestionSection C · 4 marksMedium

Write a short paragraph of five or six sentences about your plans for the coming summer vacation. Use "be going to", the present progressive for an arrangement, and "will" at least once each.

Answer: Sample: This summer I am going to learn swimming. My cousins are coming to stay with us on 1st May. We are visiting Chilika Lake together the next week; Father has booked the boat already. I am going to read two story books in English too. I think the vacation will be great fun, and I will send you some photos.

Show the explanation
  1. "am going to learn / read": intentions already decided.
  2. "are coming", "are visiting": arrangements with a date.
  3. "will be", "will send": a prediction and a promise.
  4. Marks: 1 for each correctly used form (3), 1 for a connected paragraph with correct grammar.

13QuestionSection C · 4 marksHard

Complete the dialogue with suitable forms of the verbs in brackets:
Teacher : Why _____ (you / be) late today?
Student : Sorry, sir. The bus _____ (break) down while I _____ (come) to school.
Teacher : How long _____ (you / wait) for another bus?
Student : About half an hour, sir.
Teacher : All right. The class _____ (just / start). Open your book.
Student : Thank you, sir. I _____ (not / be) late again.

Answer: Teacher : Why are you late today? Student : The bus broke down while I was coming to school. Teacher : How long did you wait for another bus? Teacher : The class has just started. Student : I won’t be late again.

Show the explanation
  1. "Why are you late today?" — a present state.
  2. "broke down while I was coming" — a short past action during a longer one: past simple + past progressive.
  3. "How long did you wait?" — the waiting is over: past simple.
  4. "has just started" — just + present perfect. "won’t be late again" — a promise: will not.
