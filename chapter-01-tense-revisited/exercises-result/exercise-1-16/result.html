iQuestion

A friend is trying to move a heavy table. You say, “———— I give you a hand?”

Rules used

  • will / shall

    A decision made at the moment of speaking, an offer, a promise or a prediction: will + V1. Shall I … ? makes an offer.

    📖 Ways of expressing the future time, 1
  1. 'Shall I … ?' is how we offer help.

Answer: “Shall I give you a hand?”

iiQuestion

“There is a lot of cleaning to do. You do the cleaning and I ——— do the drying.”

Rules used (as above)
  • will / shall

    A decision made at the moment of speaking, an offer, a promise or a prediction: will + V1. Shall I … ? makes an offer.

    📖 Ways of expressing the future time, 1
  1. A decision made while speaking, showing willingness: will.

Answer: “… You do the cleaning and I will (I’ll) do the drying.”

iiiQuestion

A friend is carrying a lot of books to the library. You are going there anyway. You say, “ I ——— take them there if you like.”

Rules used (as above)
  • will / shall

    A decision made at the moment of speaking, an offer, a promise or a prediction: will + V1. Shall I … ? makes an offer.

    📖 Ways of expressing the future time, 1
  1. An offer made on the spot: will.

Answer: “I will (I’ll) take them there if you like.”

ivQuestion

I ——— go and ask the clerk if he has a spare key.

Rules used (as above)
  • will / shall

    A decision made at the moment of speaking, an offer, a promise or a prediction: will + V1. Shall I … ? makes an offer.

    📖 Ways of expressing the future time, 1
Note: In formal British English 'I shall go' is also possible for the simple future with I/we.
  1. A decision made at the moment of speaking: will.

Answer: I will (I’ll) go and ask the clerk if he has a spare key.
