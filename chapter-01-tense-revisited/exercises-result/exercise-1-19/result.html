(1)Question

Where will you eat tonight?

Rules used

  • will / shall

    A decision made at the moment of speaking, an offer, a promise or a prediction: will + V1. Shall I … ? makes an offer.

    📖 Ways of expressing the future time, 1
  1. A plain question about the future is fine with will. (“Where are you going to eat tonight?” is also correct.)

Answer: ✓ Correct: Where will you eat tonight?

(2)Question

At home. I’ve got a cookery book, so I’ll make a curry.

Rules used

  • be going to

    An intention decided before speaking, or a future result of something we can see now: am/is/are going to + V1.

    📖 Ways of expressing the future time, 2
  1. The plan was made before speaking (he already has the cookery book): be going to.

Answer: ✗ At home. I’ve got a cookery book, so I’ll make a curry. → I’m going to make a curry.

(3)Question

Why not come to my place? I’m going to cook us both something.

Rules used (as above)
  • will / shall

    A decision made at the moment of speaking, an offer, a promise or a prediction: will + V1. Shall I … ? makes an offer.

    📖 Ways of expressing the future time, 1
  1. This is an offer made on the spot, so will is needed.

Answer: ✗ Why not come to my place? I’m going to cook us both something. → I’ll cook us both something.

(4)Question

Won’t you go to meet your Grandpa?

Rules used (as above)
  • be going to

    An intention decided before speaking, or a future result of something we can see now: am/is/are going to + V1.

    📖 Ways of expressing the future time, 2
  1. The question is about his plan or intention: be going to.

Answer: ✗ Won’t you go to meet your Grandpa? → Aren’t you going to meet your Grandpa?

(5)Question

No, he’s left for the village. I’m not going to meet him for a month or so.

Rules used (as above)
  • will / shall

    A decision made at the moment of speaking, an offer, a promise or a prediction: will + V1. Shall I … ? makes an offer.

    📖 Ways of expressing the future time, 1
  1. It is not his decision not to meet Grandpa; it is simply what will happen. ‘I’m not going to’ would sound as if he refuses.

Answer: ✗ No, he’s left for the village. I’m not going to meet him for a month or so. → I won’t meet (I won’t be seeing) him for a month or so.

(6)Question

O.K. I’m going to come round at 8 and I’ll bring some ice-cream.

Rules used (as above)
  • will / shall

    A decision made at the moment of speaking, an offer, a promise or a prediction: will + V1. Shall I … ? makes an offer.

    📖 Ways of expressing the future time, 1
  1. He accepts the invitation at this moment: will (like the second half of the line).

Answer: ✗ O.K. I’m going to come round at 8 and I’ll bring some ice-cream. → I’ll come round at 8 …

(7)Question

Fine. I’ll see you then.

Rules used (as above)
  • will / shall

    A decision made at the moment of speaking, an offer, a promise or a prediction: will + V1. Shall I … ? makes an offer.

    📖 Ways of expressing the future time, 1
  1. A spontaneous closing promise: will.

Answer: ✓ Correct: Fine. I’ll see you then.
