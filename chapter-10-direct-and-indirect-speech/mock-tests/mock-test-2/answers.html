1QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

He said, “I have been working since morning.” → He said that he _____ since that morning.

  1. (a) had been working ✓
  2. (b) has been working
  3. (c) was working
  4. (d) worked

Answer: (a) He said, “I have been working since morning.” → He said that he had been working since that morning.

Show the explanation
  1. Present perfect progressive → past perfect progressive.

2QuestionSection A · 1 marksHard

She said, “Let’s go to the cinema.” The indirect speech is:

  1. (a) She suggested that we should go to the cinema. ✓
  2. (b) She told let’s go to the cinema.
  3. (c) She said that let us go to the cinema.
  4. (d) She asked to go cinema.

Answer: (a) She suggested that we should go to the cinema.

Show the explanation
  1. "Let’s …" is a suggestion: suggest that … (should) + V1, or suggest going.

3QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

He said, “I may come tomorrow.” → He said that he _____ come the next day.

  1. (a) might ✓
  2. (b) may
  3. (c) would
  4. (d) could

Answer: (a) He said, “I may come tomorrow.” → He said that he might come the next day.

Show the explanation
  1. may → might; tomorrow → the next day.

4QuestionSection A · 1 marksHard

“Good morning, sir,” the boy said to his teacher. The indirect speech is:

  1. (a) The boy wished his teacher a good morning. ✓
  2. (b) The boy said that good morning sir.
  3. (c) The boy told good morning to his teacher.
  4. (d) The boy asked his teacher good morning.

Answer: (a) The boy wished his teacher a good morning.

Show the explanation
  1. A greeting becomes a statement with a suitable verb: wished … a good morning.

5QuestionSection A · 1 marksHard

She said, “I was cooking when he came.” → She said that she _____ when he came.

  1. (a) had been cooking ✓
  2. (b) has been cooking
  3. (c) is cooking
  4. (d) cooks

Answer: (a) She said, “I was cooking when he came.” → She said that she had been cooking when he came.

Show the explanation
  1. The past progressive usually becomes the past perfect progressive; the verb in the time clause (came) does not change.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: (ii)

6QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

He said to me, “Can you help me?” The indirect speech is:

  1. (a) He asked me if I could help him. ✓
  2. (b) He asked me can I help him.
  3. (c) He told me that could I help him?
  4. (d) He asked me if could I help him.

Answer: (a) He asked me if I could help him.

Show the explanation
  1. if + statement order (I could), can → could, no question mark.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: (J)

7QuestionSection B · 3 marksHard

Change into indirect speech:
(a) He said, “These are my books.”
(b) She said, “I bought this pen here yesterday.”
(c) He said, “I shall return next week.”

Answer: (a) He said that those were his books. (b) She said that she had bought that pen there the day before. (c) He said that he would return the following week.

Show the explanation
  1. these → those; this → that; here → there; yesterday → the day before; next week → the following week.
  2. are → were; bought → had bought; shall → would.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: (a)

8QuestionSection B · 3 marksMedium

Change into indirect speech, using a suitable reporting verb:
(a) He said, “I’m sorry. I broke the glass.”
(b) She said, “I will certainly come.”
(c) The guide said to us, “Please don’t touch the paintings.”

Answer: (a) He apologised and admitted that he had broken the glass. (b) She promised that she would come. (c) The guide requested us not to touch the paintings.

Show the explanation
  1. Choose the verb by the meaning: apologise / admit, promise, request.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: (H)

9QuestionSection B · 3 marksHard

Change into indirect speech:
(a) He said, “Alas! I have lost my purse.”
(b) She said to me, “How clever you are!”
(c) They said, “Hurrah! We have won the match.”

Answer: (a) He exclaimed with sorrow that he had lost his purse. (b) She exclaimed that I was very clever. (c) They exclaimed with joy that they had won the match.

Show the explanation
  1. Exclamations become statements: exclaimed with sorrow / joy that …; "How …!" → very.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: (L)

10QuestionSection B · 3 marksHard

Explain why the tense has not been changed:
(a) The teacher said that water boils at 100°C.
(b) He says that he is busy.
(c) Father said that he goes for a walk every morning.

Answer: (a) It is a scientific truth. (b) The reporting verb "says" is in the present. (c) It is a habitual action that is still true.

Show the explanation
  1. Rules (E) and (F): no change for truths and habits, or after a present reporting verb.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: (E)

11QuestionSection C · 4 marksHard

Report this conversation in indirect speech:
Rani : “Where are you going, Mohan?”
Mohan : “I am going to the library.”
Rani : “Will you get me a book on birds?”
Mohan : “Yes, I will.”

Answer: Rani asked Mohan where he was going. Mohan replied that he was going to the library. Rani asked him if he would get her a book on birds. Mohan replied that he would.

Show the explanation
  1. Wh-question → asked where + statement order; Yes-No question → asked if.
  2. A reply → replied that …; "Yes, I will" → he would.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: (I)

12QuestionSection C · 4 marksMedium

Change into direct speech:
(a) He told me that he was tired.
(b) She asked me if I liked music.
(c) The teacher ordered the boys to stand up.
(d) He said that he would come the next day.

Answer: (a) He said to me, “I am tired.” (b) She said to me, “Do you like music?” (c) The teacher said to the boys, “Stand up.” (d) He said, “I will come tomorrow.”

Show the explanation
  1. Work backwards: restore the exact words, the tense (was → am, liked → like, would → will), the persons and the time words (the next day → tomorrow), with inverted commas and correct punctuation.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: (C)

13QuestionSection C · 4 marksHard

Correct the errors:
(a) She asked me what is my name.
(b) He told that he was ill.
(c) The teacher told us don’t make a noise.
(d) He asked me that whether I was coming.

Answer: (a) She asked me what my name was. (b) He said that he was ill (or: He told me that he was ill). (c) The teacher told us not to make a noise. (d) He asked me whether I was coming.

Show the explanation
  1. (a) statement word order and a past tense. (b) "tell" needs a person object.
  2. (c) a negative command → not to + V1. (d) no "that" before if / whether.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: (J)
