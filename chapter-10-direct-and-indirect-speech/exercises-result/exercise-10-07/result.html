(i)Question

“Consult a doctor immediately,” he said to me.

Rules used

  • Imperatives in indirect speech

    ask / tell / order / request / advise / warn / remind + person + to + V1 (not to + V1 for a negative). 'Let’s …' → suggest that … should / suggest + V-ing.

    📖 (K) Imperatives in Indirect Speech
  1. Advice: advise + person + to + V1.

Answer: He advised me to consult a doctor immediately.

(ii)Question

“Hurry up, or you will miss the train,” he said to me.

Rules used (as above)
  • Imperatives in indirect speech

    ask / tell / order / request / advise / warn / remind + person + to + V1 (not to + V1 for a negative). 'Let’s …' → suggest that … should / suggest + V-ing.

    📖 (K) Imperatives in Indirect Speech
  1. Command + warning: tell + to + V1; will → would. (Or: He warned me to hurry up, or I would miss the train.)

Answer: He told me to hurry up, or I would miss the train.

(iii)Question

“Don’t get addicted to tea,” he said to me.

Rules used (as above)
  • Imperatives in indirect speech

    ask / tell / order / request / advise / warn / remind + person + to + V1 (not to + V1 for a negative). 'Let’s …' → suggest that … should / suggest + V-ing.

    📖 (K) Imperatives in Indirect Speech
  1. A negative imperative: not to + V1.

Answer: He advised me not to get addicted to tea.

(iv)Question

“Your mouth smells foul. Don’t open it,” he said.

Rules used (as above)
  • Imperatives in indirect speech

    ask / tell / order / request / advise / warn / remind + person + to + V1 (not to + V1 for a negative). 'Let’s …' → suggest that … should / suggest + V-ing.

    📖 (K) Imperatives in Indirect Speech
  1. The statement takes 'that'; the command takes 'not to + V1'; your → my.

Answer: He told me that my mouth smelt foul and ordered me not to open it.

(v)Question

The captain said, “Hurrah ! We have scored two goals !”

Rules used

  • Exclamations in indirect speech

    Exclamations become statements: exclaim (with joy / sorrow / surprise) that … very …; greetings and wishes become wish, greet, congratulate, bid farewell, call (someone a liar).

    📖 (L) Exclamations in Indirect Speech
  1. 'Hurrah!' shows joy; we have scored → they had scored.

Answer: The captain exclaimed with joy that they had scored two goals.
