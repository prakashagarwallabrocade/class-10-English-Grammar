1Question

“Are you leaving today ?” Maggie asked, “Yes”, I replied, “Today is the day of my departure.”
“How long will it take to reach your country ? In which part of the country do you live ?” She wanted to know. I replied, “Punjab, in the west.”
“Please, put a flower on my brother’s grave, “She requested me with tears in her eyes.

Rules used

  • Questions in indirect speech

    ask / enquire / want to know + (person) + wh-word (or if / whether for Yes-No questions) + subject + verb; no 'that', no question mark, statement word order, tense changes as usual.

    📖 (I) Questions and (J) Yes-No questions in Indirect Speech
  • Reporting verbs for statements

    Instead of 'said' use tell (+ person), state, announce, promise, report, admit, advise, complain, reply … to suit the meaning.

    📖 (H) Reporting verbs for statements
  • Imperatives in indirect speech

    ask / tell / order / request / advise / warn / remind + person + to + V1 (not to + V1 for a negative). 'Let’s …' → suggest that … should / suggest + V-ing.

    📖 (K) Imperatives in Indirect Speech
  1. Yes-No question → asked if …; Are you leaving today → I was leaving that day.
  2. 'Yes' → subject + auxiliary (I replied that I was); a continuing remark → added.
  3. Wh-questions → wanted to know how long … / in which part … with statement word order; will → would; your → my, you live → I lived.
  4. A request → requested me to + V1; my → her.

Answer: Maggie asked me if I was leaving that day. I replied that I was, and added that that was the day of my departure. She wanted to know how long it would take to reach my country and in which part of the country I lived. I replied that it was Punjab, in the west. With tears in her eyes, she requested me to put a flower on her brother’s grave.
