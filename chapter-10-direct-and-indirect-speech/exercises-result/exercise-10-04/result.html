(i)Question

Rabi said to me, “I know where you were born.”

Rules used

  • Changing to indirect speech

    Drop the inverted commas and the comma; join with 'that' (often left out); change the persons (I → he/she, you → I/me …), the tense, and the words of time and place; end with a full stop.

    📖 (D) Form of the Indirect Speech
  • Tense changes after a past reporting verb

    present simple → past simple · present progressive → past progressive · present perfect (progressive) → past perfect (progressive) · past simple → past perfect · past progressive → past perfect progressive · will → would, shall → should / would, can → could, may → might, must → must / had to / would have to.

    📖 (G) and (b) Change of modal auxiliaries
  • Reporting verbs for statements

    Instead of 'said' use tell (+ person), state, announce, promise, report, admit, advise, complain, reply … to suit the meaning.

    📖 (H) Reporting verbs for statements
  1. know → knew; you → I. 'were born' is a past fact and may stay (or: had been born).

Answer: Rabi told me that he knew where I was born.

(viii)Question

Sushil said, “We celebrate our Republic Day on 26th January every year ?”

Rules used

  • Changing to indirect speech

    Drop the inverted commas and the comma; join with 'that' (often left out); change the persons (I → he/she, you → I/me …), the tense, and the words of time and place; end with a full stop.

    📖 (D) Form of the Indirect Speech
  • When the tense does not change

    No change when the reporting verb is present (says, will say, has said), or when the words are a universal, scientific or geographical truth, a habit, or still true at the time of reporting.

    📖 (E) Reported Speech as per the Reporting Time
Note: The question mark in the printed sentence is a misprint; it is a statement.
  1. A yearly custom (habit / fact): no change of tense; we, our → they, their.

Answer: Sushil said that they celebrate their Republic Day on 26th January every year.
