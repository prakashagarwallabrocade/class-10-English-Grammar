1From direct to indirect speech10 examples

The inverted commas go, that (often optional) introduces the reported words, and pronouns change to suit the reporter.

  1. Rina said, “I am hungry.” → Rina said that she was hungry.

    statement I → she; am → was

  2. Ravi says, “I am busy.” → Ravi says that he is busy.

    present reporting verb only the pronoun changes

  3. The boy said, “My bag is heavy.” → The boy said that his bag was heavy.

    possessive change my → his

  4. She said to me, “You are late.” → She told me that I was late.

    said to → told told + person, without to

  5. We said, “We are ready.” → We said (that) we were ready.

    that is optional we stays we

  6. He said to us, “I will help you.” → He told us that he would help us.

    statement with will I → he; you → us; will → would

  7. Mother said, “Dinner is ready.” → Mother said that dinner was ready.

    statement no pronoun to change

  8. The guard said, “You can’t park here.” → The guard told us that we couldn’t park there.

    modal + place word can’t → couldn’t; here → there

  9. I said, “I like this song.” → I said that I liked that song.

    I as reporter I stays I; this → that

  10. Anil said to his sister, “I have lost your pen.” → Anil told his sister that he had lost her pen.

    present perfect → past perfect your → her

2When the tense does not change10 examples

No tense change when the reporting verb is present or future, or when the words state a truth, a habit or something still true.

  1. The teacher said, “The earth moves round the sun.” → The teacher said that the earth moves round the sun.

    universal truth present stays present

  2. He said, “Water freezes at 0 °C.” → He said that water freezes at 0 °C.

    scientific fact present stays present

  3. Gita said, “I get up at five every day.” → Gita said that she gets up at five every day.

    habit (still true) present stays present

  4. Our guide said, “Puri is on the sea coast.” → Our guide said that Puri is on the sea coast.

    geographical fact present stays present

  5. Ram says, “I want to be a pilot.” → Ram says that he wants to be a pilot.

    present reporting verb no tense change

  6. She says, “I have finished.” → She says that she has finished.

    present reporting verb has stays has

  7. The doctor will say, “You need rest.” → The doctor will say that I need rest.

    future reporting verb no tense change

  8. My father has said, “We are moving to Delhi.” → My father has said that we are moving to Delhi.

    present perfect reporting verb no tense change

  9. Mira said, “My brother is coming tonight.” (reported the same day) → Mira said that her brother is coming tonight.

    still true reported soon after, so present is fine

  10. The teacher said, “Honesty is the best policy.” → The teacher said that honesty is the best policy.

    proverb a general truth

3Tense changes after a past reporting verb10 examples

After said / told the verb usually moves one step back: present → past, past and present perfect → past perfect, will → would.

  1. He said, “I play cricket.” → He said that he played cricket.

    present simple → past simple one step back

  2. She said, “I am reading a novel.” → She said that she was reading a novel.

    present progressive → past progressive am → was

  3. They said, “We have won the match.” → They said that they had won the match.

    present perfect → past perfect have → had

  4. He said, “I have been waiting for an hour.” → He said that he had been waiting for an hour.

    present perfect progressive → past perfect progressive have been → had been

  5. Rina said, “I saw a snake.” → Rina said that she had seen a snake.

    past simple → past perfect saw → had seen

  6. He said, “I was sleeping.” → He said that he had been sleeping.

    past progressive → past perfect progressive was → had been

  7. She said, “I will come tomorrow.” → She said that she would come the next day.

    will → would tomorrow → the next day

  8. The boy said, “I am going to buy a kite.” → The boy said that he was going to buy a kite.

    am going to → was going to be going to moves back

  9. Father said, “The shop opens at nine.” → Father said that the shop opened at nine.

    present simple → past simple optional if still true

  10. She said, “I had already eaten.” → She said that she had already eaten.

    past perfect stays it cannot move further back

4Changes of time and place words10 examples

Words of nearness change when the time or place has changed: now → then, today → that day, here → there, ago → before.

  1. He said, “I am busy now.” → He said that he was busy then.

    now → then time word

  2. She said, “I have an exam today.” → She said that she had an exam that day.

    today → that day time word

  3. Ravi said, “I met her yesterday.” → Ravi said that he had met her the day before.

    yesterday → the day before also the previous day

  4. Mother said, “We will go to Puri tomorrow.” → Mother said that they would go to Puri the next day.

    tomorrow → the next day also the following day

  5. He said, “I came here two years ago.” → He said that he had come there two years before.

    ago → before here → there; came → had come

  6. She said, “I will finish it next week.” → She said that she would finish it the following week.

    next week → the following week time phrase

  7. The man said, “I lost my wallet last night.” → The man said that he had lost his wallet the previous night.

    last night → the previous night time phrase

  8. The guide said, “The temple was built here.” → The guide said that the temple had been built there.

    here → there place word

  9. He said, “I like this shirt.” → He said that he liked that shirt.

    this → that pointing word

  10. She said, “These mangoes are sweet.” → She said that those mangoes were sweet.

    these → those pointing word

5Changes of modal auxiliaries10 examples

will → would, shall → should / would, can → could, may → might, must → had to (or must); would, could, should, might, ought to do not change.

  1. He said, “I will call you.” → He said that he would call me.

    will → would you → me

  2. She said, “I can swim.” → She said that she could swim.

    can → could ability

  3. The boy said, “I may be late.” → The boy said that he might be late.

    may → might possibility

  4. Father said, “I must leave early.” → Father said that he had to leave early.

    must → had to necessity

  5. The teacher said, “You must obey the rules.” → The teacher said that we must obey the rules.

    must stays a lasting rule

  6. I said, “Shall I open the window?” → I asked if I should open the window.

    shall → should an offer

  7. She said, “I would like some tea.” → She said that she would like some tea.

    would stays no change

  8. He said, “I could run fast as a boy.” → He said that he could run fast as a boy.

    could stays no change

  9. The doctor said, “You should exercise daily.” → The doctor said that I should exercise daily.

    should stays advice

  10. Mother said, “You ought to respect your elders.” → Mother said that I ought to respect my elders.

    ought to stays duty

6Changes of pronouns and determiners10 examples

First and second person words change to suit the reporter and the listener; this / these become that / those.

  1. Rita said, “I am tired.” → Rita said that she was tired.

    I → she the speaker is Rita

  2. The boys said, “We are hungry.” → The boys said that they were hungry.

    we → they the speakers are the boys

  3. He said to me, “You are right.” → He told me that I was right.

    you → I the listener is me

  4. She said to Ravi, “Your book is here.” → She told Ravi that his book was there.

    your → his the listener is Ravi

  5. Ajay said, “This is my bicycle.” → Ajay said that that was his bicycle.

    my → his this → that too

  6. The girls said to us, “We will help you.” → The girls told us that they would help us.

    we → they; you → us speakers and listeners change

  7. She said, “I hurt myself.” → She said that she had hurt herself.

    myself → herself reflexive pronoun

  8. He said, “This is our house.” → He said that that was their house.

    this → that; our → their determiner and pronoun

  9. I said to him, “I will meet you.” → I told him that I would meet him.

    you → him I stays I

  10. The teacher said, “These are your books.” → The teacher said that those were our books.

    these → those; your → our the listeners were us

7Reporting verbs for statements10 examples

Instead of said, choose a verb that shows the speaker’s purpose — told, replied, promised, admitted, complained, explained, warned, agreed.

  1. He said, “Yes, I broke the vase.” → He admitted that he had broken the vase.

    admitted owning up

  2. She said, “I will return the book tomorrow.” → She promised that she would return the book the next day.

    promised a promise

  3. The customer said, “The food is cold.” → The customer complained that the food was cold.

    complained unhappy

  4. Gopal said, “No, I am not coming.” → Gopal replied that he was not coming.

    replied an answer

  5. The teacher said, “The test is on Monday.” → The teacher announced that the test was on Monday.

    announced news for everyone

  6. The guard said, “The bridge is unsafe.” → The guard warned us that the bridge was unsafe.

    warned a danger

  7. She said, “The train was late, so I missed the class.” → She explained that she had missed the class because the train had been late.

    explained giving a reason

  8. He said, “I didn’t take the money.” → He denied taking the money.

    denied deny + -ing

  9. Rina said, “Yes, you are right.” → Rina agreed that I was right.

    agreed agreement

  10. The boy said, “I have done my homework.” → The boy told the teacher that he had done his homework.

    told told + person

8Wh-questions in indirect speech10 examples

Use asked / wanted to know + the question word + statement word order; no question mark, no do / did.

  1. He said to me, “Where do you live?” → He asked me where I lived.

    where do you live → I lived

  2. She said, “What is your name?” → She asked me what my name was.

    what is your name → my name was

  3. The teacher said, “Why are you late?” → The teacher asked me why I was late.

    why subject before verb

  4. Mother said, “When will you come back?” → Mother asked me when I would come back.

    when will → would

  5. He said, “Who broke the window?” → He asked who had broken the window.

    who (subject) broke → had broken

  6. The stranger said, “How far is the station?” → The stranger asked how far the station was.

    how far is → was, moved to the end

  7. I said to her, “Which dress did you buy?” → I asked her which dress she had bought.

    which did you buy → she had bought

  8. She said, “How are you?” → She asked me how I was.

    how statement order

  9. The officer said, “What have you done?” → The officer asked me what I had done.

    what have → had

  10. He said to the boy, “Whose bag is this?” → He asked the boy whose bag that was.

    whose this → that

9Yes / No questions in indirect speech10 examples

Use asked + if / whether + statement word order.

  1. He said to me, “Are you ready?” → He asked me if I was ready.

    if are you → I was

  2. She said, “Do you like tea?” → She asked me whether I liked tea.

    whether do you like → I liked

  3. The teacher said, “Have you finished?” → The teacher asked if we had finished.

    if have you → we had

  4. Mother said, “Did you eat your lunch?” → Mother asked me if I had eaten my lunch.

    if did you eat → I had eaten

  5. He said, “Can you help me?” → He asked me if I could help him.

    if can → could

  6. I said to Rina, “Will you come to the party?” → I asked Rina whether she would come to the party.

    whether will → would

  7. The shopkeeper said, “Is this your bag?” → The shopkeeper asked me if that was my bag.

    if this → that

  8. She said, “Shall I bring an umbrella?” → She asked whether she should bring an umbrella.

    whether shall → should

  9. He said, “Are you coming or not?” → He asked whether I was coming or not.

    whether … or not whether, not if

  10. The doctor said, “Do you sleep well?” → The doctor asked me if I slept well.

    if do you sleep → I slept

10Commands, requests and advice10 examples

Use told / ordered / requested / advised / begged + person + to + verb; not to + verb for a negative.

  1. The teacher said to us, “Sit down.” → The teacher told us to sit down.

    told + to-verb a command

  2. He said to me, “Please lend me your pen.” → He requested me to lend him my pen.

    requested + to-verb please → requested

  3. Mother said, “Don’t play in the sun.” → Mother told me not to play in the sun.

    not to + verb a negative command

  4. The officer said to the soldiers, “Fire!” → The officer ordered the soldiers to fire.

    ordered + to-verb a military command

  5. The doctor said to him, “Take this medicine twice a day.” → The doctor advised him to take that medicine twice a day.

    advised + to-verb this → that

  6. The beggar said, “Please give me some food.” → The beggar begged me to give him some food.

    begged + to-verb a humble request

  7. She said to the children, “Don’t make a noise.” → She told the children not to make a noise.

    not to + verb negative

  8. He said, “Let’s go for a swim.” → He suggested that we should go for a swim.

    suggested that let’s → suggested

  9. The coach said, “Practise every day.” → The coach advised us to practise every day.

    advised + to-verb advice

  10. The guard said to the visitors, “Don’t touch the paintings.” → The guard warned the visitors not to touch the paintings.

    warned + not to-verb a warning

11Exclamations and wishes10 examples

Use exclaimed with joy / sorrow / surprise, cried, wished, thanked or prayed, and a statement.

  1. She said, “What a lovely garden!” → She exclaimed with joy that it was a very lovely garden.

    exclaimed with joy What a → a very

  2. He said, “How tall the tower is!” → He exclaimed with surprise that the tower was very tall.

    exclaimed with surprise How + adjective → very + adjective

  3. The old man said, “Alas! My son is dead.” → The old man exclaimed with sorrow that his son was dead.

    exclaimed with sorrow Alas shows sorrow

  4. They said, “Hurrah! We have won.” → They exclaimed with joy that they had won.

    exclaimed with joy Hurrah shows joy

  5. Rina said, “Oh! I have cut my finger.” → Rina cried out in pain that she had cut her finger.

    cried out in pain Oh shows pain here

  6. He said to me, “Good morning!” → He wished me good morning.

    wished a greeting

  7. She said, “Thank you!” → She thanked me.

    thanked thanks

  8. The old woman said, “May God bless you!” → The old woman prayed that God might bless me.

    prayed that a blessing

  9. He said, “Bravo! Well played!” → He applauded the player, saying that he had played well.

    applauded praise

  10. She said to her friend, “Happy birthday!” → She wished her friend a happy birthday.

    wished a greeting
