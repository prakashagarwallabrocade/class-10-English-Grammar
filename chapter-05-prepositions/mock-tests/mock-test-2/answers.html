1QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

He has lived here _____ 2015.

  1. (a) for
  2. (b) since ✓
  3. (c) from
  4. (d) in

Answer: (b) He has lived here since 2015.

Show the explanation
  1. 2015 is the point of time the living began; with the present perfect: since.

2QuestionSection A · 1 marksHard

Choose the correct sentence.

  1. (a) I will see you on next Monday.
  2. (b) I will see you next Monday. ✓
  3. (c) I will see you in next Monday.
  4. (d) I will see you at next Monday.

Answer: (b) I will see you next Monday.

Show the explanation
  1. No preposition is used before next, last, this, every (but "on Monday next" is possible).

3QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

The answer was written _____ ink.

  1. (a) with
  2. (b) in ✓
  3. (c) by
  4. (d) from

Answer: (b) The answer was written in ink.

Show the explanation
  1. The material used for writing takes "in" (in ink, in pencil); the tool takes "with" (with a pen).

Choose the pair that fills the blanks: "He complained _____ the manager _____ the poor service."

  1. (a) to … about ✓
  2. (b) with … for
  3. (c) at … of
  4. (d) to … for

Answer: (a) to … about

Show the explanation
  1. complain to a person about a matter (the book’s example: He complained to the headmaster about lack of good drinking water).

5QuestionSection A · 1 marksHard

Choose the pair that fills the blanks: "The shop remains closed _____ Saturday evening _____ Monday morning."

  1. (a) from … to ✓
  2. (b) since … to
  3. (c) between … to
  4. (d) by … till

Answer: (a) from … to

Show the explanation
  1. The start and end of a period: from … to / till / until. ("between" would need "and".)

6QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

We reached the station _____ time to catch the train.

  1. (a) in ✓
  2. (b) on
  3. (c) at
  4. (d) by

Answer: (a) We reached the station in time to catch the train.

Show the explanation
  1. "in time (to …)" = early enough; "on time" = at the scheduled time.

7QuestionSection B · 3 marksHard

Explain the difference, with an example of each:
(a) between / among
(b) on time / in time
(c) die of / die from

Answer: (a) between two (The road runs between the school and the hospital); among three or more (Distribute the sweets among the children). (b) on time = at the scheduled time (The train arrived on time); in time = early enough (He arrived in time to see his friend off). (c) die of an illness (He died of cholera); die from another cause such as a wound or overeating (He died from his wounds).

Show the explanation
  1. One mark for each pair explained with a correct example.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: 2.11

8QuestionSection B · 3 marksMedium

Fill in the blanks with over, across or below:
(a) The thief jumped _____ the wall and ran away.
(b) We walked _____ the bridge to the other bank.
(c) The sun sinks _____ the horizon in the evening.

Answer: (a) jumped over the wall (b) walked across the bridge (c) sinks below the horizon

Show the explanation
  1. (a) over = to the other side of something high. (b) across = from one side to the other. (c) below = lower than.

9QuestionSection B · 3 marksHard

Correct the errors:
(a) He has been ill since three days.
(b) I am waiting for him from morning.
(c) She was born in 15th August.

Answer: (a) He has been ill for three days. (b) I have been waiting for him since morning. (c) She was born on 15th August.

Show the explanation
  1. (a) a period: for. (b) a point of time continuing to now: since, with the perfect. (c) a date: on.

11QuestionSection C · 4 marksHard

Fill in the blanks with suitable prepositions (½ mark each):
Last Sunday we went _____ a picnic _____ Chilika. We left home _____ 6 a.m. and travelled _____ bus. _____ the way we stopped _____ a small hotel for breakfast. We reached the lake _____ two hours, and then we went _____ a boat ride.

Answer: Last Sunday we went on a picnic to Chilika. We left home at 6 a.m. and travelled by bus. On the way we stopped at a small hotel for breakfast. We reached the lake in two hours, and then we went on a boat ride.

Show the explanation
  1. go on a picnic / on a ride; to + destination; at + clock time; by + vehicle without an article.
  2. on the way (fixed phrase); at + a particular place where we stopped; in + a period at the end of which something happens.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: 3

12QuestionSection C · 4 marksMedium

Use each of these multi-word prepositions in a sentence of your own: (a) in spite of (b) because of (c) instead of (d) according to.

Answer: Sample: (a) In spite of the rain, the match went on. (b) The school was closed because of the flood. (c) He drank milk instead of tea. (d) According to the weather report, it will be sunny tomorrow.

Show the explanation
  1. Each multi-word preposition is followed by a noun or noun phrase. One mark for each correct sentence.
