1QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

The fire was put _____ by the firemen.

  1. (a) out ✓
  2. (b) off
  3. (c) down
  4. (d) up

Answer: (a) The fire was put out by the firemen.

Show the explanation
  1. put out = extinguish.

2QuestionSection A · 1 marksHard

Choose the correct sentence.

  1. (a) I look forward to meet you.
  2. (b) I look forward to meeting you. ✓
  3. (c) I look forward meeting you.
  4. (d) I look forward for meeting you.

Answer: (b) I look forward to meeting you.

Show the explanation
  1. In "look forward to", "to" is a preposition, so it is followed by a noun or a verb-ing (the book: We are looking forward to seeing you again).

3QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

In "She turned down the offer", "turned down" means:

  1. (a) rejected ✓
  2. (b) lowered
  3. (c) accepted
  4. (d) delayed

Answer: (a) rejected

Show the explanation
  1. turn down = reject (the meaning cannot be guessed from "turn" and "down").

4QuestionSection A · 1 marksHard

Choose the correct sentence.

  1. (a) She looked them after.
  2. (b) She looked after them. ✓
  3. (c) She after looked them.
  4. (d) She looked after it them.

Answer: (b) She looked after them.

Show the explanation
  1. "look after" is a prepositional verb (not separable): even a pronoun object comes after "after".

5QuestionSection A · 1 marksHard

In "The battery has run down", "run down" means:

  1. (a) became weak ✓
  2. (b) criticised
  3. (c) knocked over
  4. (d) ran away

Answer: (a) became weak

Show the explanation
  1. Used intransitively, run down = become weak; used transitively about a person (running down his neighbours) it means criticise.

6QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

He was ill for a month, so he could not _____ the class.

  1. (a) keep up with ✓
  2. (b) keep on
  3. (c) keep off
  4. (d) keep back

Answer: (a) He was ill for a month, so he could not keep up with the class.

Show the explanation
  1. keep up with = go forward at an equal pace with.

7QuestionSection B · 3 marksHard

Write sentences to show two different meanings of each phrasal verb:
(a) make up
(b) take off

Answer: (a) make up — They have made up their quarrel (settle). / He made up a story to escape punishment (invent). (b) take off — The aeroplane took off smoothly (leave the ground). / He took off his cap (remove).

Show the explanation
  1. Many phrasal verbs have more than one meaning; the sentence shows which one is meant. ¾ mark for each correct sentence.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: Common Phrasal

8QuestionSection B · 3 marksMedium

Replace the underlined words with phrasal verbs:
(a) The meeting has been postponed.
(b) Please continue your work.
(c) The child resembles his grandfather.

Answer: (a) The meeting has been put off. (b) Please carry on with your work. (c) The child takes after his grandfather.

Show the explanation
  1. postpone = put off; continue = carry on (with); resemble = take after.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: Phrasal Verbs

9QuestionSection B · 3 marksHard

Use each phrasal verb in two sentences, once intransitively and once transitively:
(a) turn out
(b) give out

Answer: (a) Intransitive: The whole village turned out to welcome the team. Transitive: This factory turns out five hundred bicycles a day. (b) Intransitive: His patience gave out. Transitive: The teacher gave out the question papers.

Show the explanation
  1. Intransitive: no object (came out; became exhausted). Transitive: with an object (produces; distributed).
← Back to the question 📖 Section: Transitive

10QuestionSection B · 3 marksHard

Replace the noun phrase with a pronoun and put it in the right place:
(a) I called on my teacher.
(b) He threw away the old shoes.
(c) We are looking into the complaint.

Answer: (a) I called on him. (b) He threw them away. (c) We are looking into it.

Show the explanation
  1. (a) and (c) are prepositional verbs: the pronoun follows the preposition.
  2. (b) "throw away" is separable: the pronoun goes between verb and particle.

11QuestionSection C · 4 marksHard

Complete the passage with suitable phrasal verbs from the box (½ mark each):
[ broke out, turned up, put out, called off, set off, looked into, blown up, get over ]
Last night a fire _____ in our street. The fire brigade _____ within ten minutes, and the firemen _____ the fire in an hour. Nobody was hurt, but the wedding party next door was _____. The guests _____ for home early. The next morning the police _____ the cause. They found that a gas cylinder had _____. The family is still trying to _____ the shock.

Answer: Last night a fire broke out in our street. The fire brigade turned up within ten minutes, and the firemen put out the fire in an hour. Nobody was hurt, but the wedding party next door was called off. The guests set off for home early. The next morning the police looked into the cause. They found that a gas cylinder had blown up. The family is still trying to get over the shock.

Show the explanation
  1. broke out = started suddenly; turned up = arrived; put out = extinguished; called off = cancelled.
  2. set off = started a journey; looked into = investigated; blown up = exploded; get over = recover from.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: Common Phrasal

12QuestionSection C · 4 marksMedium

Write a short paragraph (4–5 sentences) about your morning routine, using at least four phrasal verbs.

Answer: Sample: I get up at six every morning. After a bath I put on my school uniform. I look after my little sister till my mother is ready. At half past seven I set off for school with my friends.

Show the explanation
  1. One mark for each phrasal verb used correctly in a connected paragraph.

13QuestionSection C · 4 marksHard

Correct the errors:
(a) He gave up it.
(b) I am looking forward to see you.
(c) Please turn off it.
(d) We have run out sugar.

Answer: (a) He gave it up. (b) I am looking forward to seeing you. (c) Please turn it off. (d) We have run out of sugar.

Show the explanation
  1. (a) and (c): a pronoun object goes between the verb and the particle.
  2. (b) "look forward to" + verb-ing. (d) The phrasal-prepositional verb is "run out of".
