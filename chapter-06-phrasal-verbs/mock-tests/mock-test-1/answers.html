1QuestionSection A · 1 marksEasy

The meeting was _____ because of the rain.

  1. (a) called off ✓
  2. (b) called on
  3. (c) called up
  4. (d) called for

Answer: (a) The meeting was called off because of the rain.

Show the explanation
  1. call off = cancel. (call on = visit; call up = telephone.)

2QuestionSection A · 1 marksEasy

It is dark in here. Please _____ the light.

  1. (a) turn on ✓
  2. (b) turn down
  3. (c) turn off
  4. (d) turn out

Answer: (a) It is dark in here. Please turn on the light.

Show the explanation
  1. turn on = switch on; it is dark, so the light must be switched on.

5QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

In "They have put off the meeting", "put off" means:

  1. (a) postponed ✓
  2. (b) extinguished
  3. (c) suppressed
  4. (d) accommodated

Answer: (a) postponed

Show the explanation
  1. put off = postpone; put out = extinguish; put down = suppress; put up = accommodate.

Choose the correct sentence.

  1. (a) He gave up it.
  2. (b) He gave it up. ✓
  3. (c) He up gave it.
  4. (d) He gave it upon.

Answer: (b) He gave it up.

  1. A pronoun object goes between the verb and the particle.

8QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

In which sentence is the phrasal verb intransitive?

  1. (a) My car broke down. ✓
  2. (b) They set up a school.
  3. (c) Please turn on the fan.
  4. (d) He looked up the word.

Answer: (a) My car broke down.

Show the explanation
  1. "broke down" has no object; the others have objects (a school, the fan, the word).

11QuestionSection B · 2 marksEasy

Replace the underlined words with phrasal verbs, using the verbs in brackets:
(a) The workers cancelled the strike. (call)
(b) He could not understand the notice. (make)

Answer: (a) The workers called off the strike. (b) He could not make out the notice.

Show the explanation
  1. call off = cancel; make out = understand.

12QuestionSection B · 2 marksEasy

Replace the noun phrase with a pronoun and put it in the right place:
(a) Switch off the fan.
(b) He picked up the books.

Answer: (a) Switch it off. (b) He picked them up.

Show the explanation
  1. The pronoun goes between the verb and the particle.

13QuestionSection B · 2 marksMedium

Write NS if the particle cannot be separated; otherwise rewrite with the particle after the object:
(a) She is looking for her keys.
(b) They blew up the old bridge.

Answer: (a) NS (b) They blew the old bridge up.

Show the explanation
  1. (a) look for is a prepositional verb: its object must follow "for".
  2. (b) blow up has an adverb particle, which can move after the object.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: Transitive

14QuestionSection B · 2 marksEasy

Fill in the particles:
(a) The thief broke _____ the house at night.
(b) Carry _____ with your work.

Answer: (a) The thief broke into the house at night. (b) Carry on with your work.

Show the explanation
  1. break into = enter by force; carry on = continue.

15QuestionSection B · 2 marksMedium

Give the meaning of:
(a) set up
(b) get over

Answer: (a) set up = start, establish (b) get over = overcome

Show the explanation
  1. He set up a school for poor children. He got over his difficulties.

16QuestionSection C · 5 marksEasy

Fill in the blanks with the phrasal verbs in the box: [ got up, put on, set out, came across, looked after ]
Yesterday I _____ early. I _____ my uniform and _____ for school. On the way I _____ an injured puppy. I took it home and _____ it.

Answer: Yesterday I got up early. I put on my uniform and set out for school. On the way I came across an injured puppy. I took it home and looked after it.

Show the explanation
  1. got up = left bed; put on = wore; set out = started a journey; came across = met by chance; looked after = took care of.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: Common Phrasal

17QuestionSection C · 5 marksMedium

Use each of these phrasal verbs in a sentence of your own: give up, look forward to, put up with, bring up, turn down.

Answer: Sample: My uncle has given up smoking. I am looking forward to the summer holidays. I cannot put up with loud music at night. She was brought up by her grandparents. The manager turned down my request for leave.

Show the explanation
  1. One mark for each sentence that shows the meaning correctly: give up = stop; look forward to = expect eagerly; put up with = tolerate; bring up = rear; turn down = reject.
