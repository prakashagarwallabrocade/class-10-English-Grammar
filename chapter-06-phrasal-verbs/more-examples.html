1Phrasal verbs with easy meanings10 examples

The meaning comes straight from the verb and the particle.

  1. Please sit down.

    sit down take a seat

  2. The teacher asked us to stand up.

    stand up rise to your feet

  3. She came in and closed the door.

    come in enter

  4. The cat jumped out of the basket.

    jump out leave by jumping

  5. Go back to your seat.

    go back return

  6. He looked up at the stars.

    look up raise one’s eyes

  7. The balloon flew away.

    fly away move off in the air

  8. Please take off your shoes before entering the temple.

    take off (clothes) remove

  9. The price of onions has gone up.

    go up rise

  10. Climb down carefully from the ladder.

    climb down descend

2Phrasal verbs with idiomatic meanings10 examples

The meaning cannot be guessed from the parts; most can be replaced by one word.

  1. The meeting was put off till Monday.

    put off = postpone not about putting anything

  2. Firemen soon put out the fire.

    put out = extinguish one-word meaning

  3. I came across an old photograph in the drawer.

    come across = find by chance idiomatic

  4. She takes after her grandmother.

    take after = resemble looks or behaves like

  5. He has given up sugar for his health.

    give up = stop stop doing or having

  6. Don’t give in to bad habits.

    give in = surrender yield

  7. We ran out of milk this morning.

    run out of = have none left three-part verb

  8. The plane took off at 6 am.

    take off = leave the ground different from take off your shoes

  9. Can you make out what he is saying?

    make out = understand idiomatic

  10. The two friends fell out over a small matter.

    fall out = quarrel idiomatic

3Transitive and intransitive use10 examples

An intransitive phrasal verb has no object; a transitive one has an object. Some can be both, sometimes with a change of meaning.

  1. The bus broke down on the highway.

    intransitive no object

  2. Prices came down after the harvest.

    intransitive no object

  3. The children grew up in a village.

    intransitive no object

  4. Please turn off the fan.

    transitive object = the fan

  5. They set up a library in the village.

    transitive object = a library

  6. He looked up the meaning in the dictionary.

    transitive object = the meaning

  7. My watch has run down. / Don’t run down your friends.

    both intransitive = stopped working; transitive = criticise

  8. Crowds turned out to see the parade. / The factory turns out 500 bicycles a day.

    both came out; produces

  9. Her strength gave out. / The teacher gave out the question papers.

    both became exhausted; distributed

  10. The taxi pulled up at the gate. / Mother pulled up the weeds.

    both stopped; removed (object = the weeds)

4Where the object goes10 examples

With most transitive phrasal verbs a noun object can go before or after the particle, but a pronoun object must go between the verb and the particle.

  1. Switch on the light. / Switch the light on.

    noun object either position

  2. Switch it on.

    pronoun object not switch on it

  3. Please fill in this form. / Please fill this form in.

    noun object either position

  4. Please fill it in today.

    pronoun object between verb and particle

  5. She picked up the baby. / She picked him up.

    noun / pronoun object pronoun goes in the middle

  6. Write down these words. / Write them down.

    noun / pronoun object pronoun goes in the middle

  7. Don’t throw away the old newspapers; don’t throw them away.

    noun / pronoun object pronoun goes in the middle

  8. I will look after your dog. / I will look after it.

    prepositional verb the object always comes after the preposition

  9. She ran into an old friend at the market. / She ran into him.

    prepositional verb object after into

  10. We are looking forward to the holidays. / We are looking forward to them.

    three-part verb the object always comes at the end

5Prepositional and phrasal-prepositional verbs10 examples

A prepositional verb is verb + preposition (look at, listen to); a phrasal-prepositional verb is verb + adverb + preposition (put up with).

  1. Please listen to me.

    verb + preposition listen to

  2. Who will look after the baby?

    verb + preposition look after = take care of

  3. The police are looking into the matter.

    verb + preposition look into = investigate

  4. I called on my teacher yesterday.

    verb + preposition call on = visit

  5. The thieves broke into the shop.

    verb + preposition break into = enter by force

  6. I can’t put up with this noise any longer.

    verb + adverb + preposition put up with = tolerate

  7. Walk slowly so that I can keep up with you.

    verb + adverb + preposition keep up with = stay level with

  8. He got away with cheating in the test.

    verb + adverb + preposition get away with = escape punishment

  9. We must do away with old customs that harm people.

    verb + adverb + preposition do away with = abolish

  10. She came up with a clever idea.

    verb + adverb + preposition come up with = think of

6One verb, many particles10 examples

The same verb gives different meanings with different particles.

  1. She looked for her lost key.

    look for = search look + for

  2. Children should look up to their parents.

    look up to = respect look + up + to

  3. Can you bring up this point at the meeting?

    bring up = raise also means rear a child

  4. The publisher has brought out a new dictionary.

    bring out = publish bring + out

  5. Please carry on with your work.

    carry on = continue carry + on

  6. The doctor carried out the operation successfully.

    carry out = perform carry + out

  7. Our car broke down on the way to Puri.

    break down = stop working break + down

  8. The war broke out in 1939.

    break out = begin suddenly break + out

  9. Hold on, I am coming.

    hold on = wait hold + on

  10. The robbers held up the bank.

    hold up = rob by force also means delay
