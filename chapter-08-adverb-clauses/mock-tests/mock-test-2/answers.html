1QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

"Had I known it, I would have helped you." The first part means:

  1. (a) If I had known it ✓
  2. (b) Because I knew it
  3. (c) Although I knew it
  4. (d) When I know it

Answer: (a) If I had known it

Show the explanation
  1. Putting "had" before the subject is another way of writing a third-conditional if-clause.

2QuestionSection A · 1 marksHard

Choose the correct sentence.

  1. (a) If I will see him, I will tell him.
  2. (b) If I see him, I will tell him. ✓
  3. (c) If I saw him, I will tell him.
  4. (d) If I had seen him, I will tell him.

Answer: (b) If I see him, I will tell him.

Show the explanation
  1. First conditional: the if-clause takes the present simple (not will), the main clause will + V1.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: rule:3a

3QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

In "She speaks as if she were a queen", the adverb clause is of:

  1. (a) manner ✓
  2. (b) reason
  3. (c) time
  4. (d) condition

Answer: (a) manner

Show the explanation
  1. "as if" tells how she speaks.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: rule:1a

4QuestionSection A · 1 marksHard

Which sentence has an adverb clause of concession?

  1. (a) He came because he was invited.
  2. (b) He came although he was not invited. ✓
  3. (c) He came as soon as he was invited.
  4. (d) He came so that he could meet us.

Answer: (b) He came although he was not invited.

Show the explanation
  1. "although" shows that the action happened in spite of something; the others show reason, time and purpose.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: rule:2a

5QuestionSection A · 1 marksHard

If you had studied, you _____ passed.

  1. (a) would have ✓
  2. (b) will have
  3. (c) would
  4. (d) had

Answer: (a) If you had studied, you would have passed.

Show the explanation
  1. Third conditional: if + past perfect, would have + V3.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: rule:3a

6QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

In "We left early in order that we might get seats", the adverb clause shows:

  1. (a) purpose ✓
  2. (b) reason
  3. (c) time
  4. (d) result

Answer: (a) purpose

Show the explanation
  1. "in order that" (like "so that") introduces a clause of purpose.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: rule:1a

7QuestionSection B · 3 marksHard

Fill in the blanks with suitable forms of the verbs in brackets:
(a) If water _____ (heat) to 100°C, it boils.
(b) If I _____ (be) the Prime Minister, I would build more schools.
(c) If you had told me earlier, I _____ (help) you.

Answer: (a) If water is heated to 100°C, it boils. (b) If I were the Prime Minister, I would build more schools. (c) If you had told me earlier, I would have helped you.

Show the explanation
  1. (a) zero conditional (a scientific fact), in the passive. (b) second conditional: were. (c) third conditional: would have + V3.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: rule:3a

8QuestionSection B · 3 marksMedium

Pick out the adverb clause and name its kind:
(a) Wherever he goes, he makes friends.
(b) I couldn’t sleep because the dog was barking.
(c) She left before the meeting ended.

Answer: (a) Wherever he goes — place (b) because the dog was barking — reason (c) before the meeting ended — time

Show the explanation
  1. Ask where? why? when? about the main verb.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: rule:1a

9QuestionSection B · 3 marksHard

Explain the difference in meaning:
(a) If it rains, the match will be cancelled.
(b) If it rained, the match would be cancelled.

Answer: (a) First conditional: rain is a real possibility, and then the match will be cancelled. (b) Second conditional: the speaker thinks rain is unlikely or is only imagining it.

Show the explanation
  1. Present + will = an open real condition (50-50 chance); past + would = a hypothetical condition.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: rule:3a

10QuestionSection B · 3 marksHard

Rewrite as directed:
(a) Unless you apologise, I won’t talk to you. (Use "if … not".)
(b) Because it was late, we took a taxi. (Make it compound with "so".)
(c) In spite of being ill, he came to school. (Use "although".)

Answer: (a) If you do not apologise, I won’t talk to you. (b) It was late, so we took a taxi. (c) Although he was ill, he came to school.

Show the explanation
  1. (a) unless = if … not. (b) The reason clause becomes an independent clause joined by "so".
  2. (c) The phrase becomes a finite clause of concession.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: rule:1a

11QuestionSection C · 4 marksHard

Write a conditional sentence for each situation:
(a) You have no wings, but you want to fly.
(b) You did not take an umbrella, and you got wet.
(c) Ice always melts when it is heated.
(d) It may rain tomorrow; then the picnic will be cancelled.

Answer: (a) If I had wings, I would fly. (b) If I had taken an umbrella, I would not have got wet. (c) If ice is heated, it melts. (d) If it rains tomorrow, the picnic will be cancelled.

Show the explanation
  1. (a) imaginary: second conditional. (b) unfulfilled past: third conditional.
  2. (c) a fact: zero conditional. (d) a real possibility: first conditional.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: rule:3a

12QuestionSection C · 4 marksMedium

Write four sentences of your own containing adverb clauses of (a) time (b) place (c) reason (d) purpose.

Answer: Sample: (a) I will call you when I reach Puri. (b) Put the book back where you found it. (c) We stayed indoors because it was very hot. (d) She saves money so that she can buy a bicycle.

Show the explanation
  1. One mark for each correct adverb clause of the kind asked for.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: rule:1a

13QuestionSection C · 4 marksHard

Find the four adverb clauses in this passage and name their kinds:
When Ravi reached the station, the train had left. He waited because there was no other way home. He sat where he could see the clock. Although he was tired, he did not sleep.

Answer: When Ravi reached the station — time; because there was no other way home — reason; where he could see the clock — place; Although he was tired — concession

Show the explanation
  1. Each clause begins with a subordinating word and cannot stand alone; ask when? why? where? and in spite of what?
← Back to the question 📖 Section: rule:1a
