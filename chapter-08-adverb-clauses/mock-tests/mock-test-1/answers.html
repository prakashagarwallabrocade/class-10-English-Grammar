1QuestionSection A · 1 marksEasy

I will wait here _____ you come back.

  1. (a) until ✓
  2. (b) unless
  3. (c) though
  4. (d) so that

Answer: (a) I will wait here until you come back.

Show the explanation
  1. An adverb clause of time: until (up to the time when).

2QuestionSection A · 1 marksEasy

He stayed at home _____ he was ill.

  1. (a) because ✓
  2. (b) although
  3. (c) unless
  4. (d) so that

Answer: (a) He stayed at home because he was ill.

Show the explanation
  1. The clause gives the reason: because.

3QuestionSection A · 1 marksEasy

_____ it was raining, we went out.

  1. (a) Although ✓
  2. (b) Because
  3. (c) If
  4. (d) So that

Answer: (a) Although it was raining, we went out.

Show the explanation
  1. Going out in the rain is unexpected: a clause of concession (although).

  2. (b) because
  3. (c) although
  4. (d) unless

Answer: (a) Work hard so that you may pass.

Show the explanation
  1. The clause gives the purpose: so that.

5QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

"If you heat ice, it melts." This sentence is a:

  1. (a) zero conditional ✓
  2. (b) first conditional
  3. (c) second conditional
  4. (d) third conditional

Answer: (a) zero conditional

Show the explanation
  1. A general / scientific truth with the present simple in both clauses.

6QuestionSection A · 1 marksEasy

She sings _____ a nightingale does.

  1. (a) as ✓
  2. (b) than
  3. (c) so
  4. (d) if

Answer: (a) She sings as a nightingale does.

Show the explanation
  1. The clause shows the manner of singing: as.

7QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

If I _____ a bird, I would fly.

  1. (a) were ✓
  2. (b) am
  3. (c) will be
  4. (d) have been

Answer: (a) If I were a bird, I would fly.

Show the explanation
  1. An imaginary condition (second conditional) takes the past form; "were" is used with all subjects.

8QuestionSection A · 1 marksEasy

Ravi is taller _____ his brother is.

  1. (a) than ✓
  2. (b) as
  3. (c) so
  4. (d) that

Answer: (a) Ravi is taller than his brother is.

Show the explanation
  1. A comparative (taller) is followed by "than": a clause of comparison.

9QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

What kind of adverb clause is in "I’ll go where you go"?

  1. (a) time
  2. (b) place ✓
  3. (c) manner
  4. (d) reason

Answer: (b) place

Show the explanation
  1. "where you go" tells the place.

10QuestionSection A · 1 marksMedium

If you _____ hard, you will pass.

  1. (a) work ✓
  2. (b) worked
  3. (c) had worked
  4. (d) would work

Answer: (a) If you work hard, you will pass.

Show the explanation
  1. First conditional: if + present simple, will + V1. The present tense here has a future meaning.

11QuestionSection B · 2 marksEasy

Complete each sentence with an adverb clause:
(a) I will phone you when _____ .
(b) She smiled as if _____ .

Answer: Sample: (a) I will phone you when I reach home. (b) She smiled as if she knew the answer.

Show the explanation
  1. (a) time: the present simple is used for the future after "when". (b) manner.

12QuestionSection B · 2 marksEasy

Name the kind of adverb clause:
(a) Unless you hurry, you will miss the bus.
(b) Though he is rich, he is humble.

Answer: (a) condition (b) concession

Show the explanation
  1. (a) "Unless" = if … not. (b) "Though" shows a contrast.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: rule:1a

13QuestionSection B · 2 marksMedium

Combine into complex sentences, using the word in brackets:
(a) It was dark. We went home. (as)
(b) He is poor. He is honest. (though)

Answer: (a) As it was dark, we went home. (b) Though he is poor, he is honest.

Show the explanation
  1. (a) reason; (b) concession. The adverb clause comes first, followed by a comma.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: rule:1a

14QuestionSection B · 2 marksEasy

Fill in the blanks with the correct forms of the verbs in brackets:
(a) If it rains, we _____ (stay) at home.
(b) If I had money, I _____ (buy) a bicycle.

Answer: (a) If it rains, we will stay at home. (b) If I had money, I would buy a bicycle.

Show the explanation
  1. (a) first conditional: will + V1. (b) second conditional: would + V1.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: rule:3a

15QuestionSection B · 2 marksMedium

Rewrite without "if":
(a) If I were rich, I would travel round the world.
(b) If he had come, I would have helped him.

Answer: (a) Were I rich, I would travel round the world. (b) Had he come, I would have helped him.

Show the explanation
  1. Drop "if" and put "were" / "had" before the subject (Were wishes horses … / Had he arrived in time …).
← Back to the question 📖 Section: rule:3a

16QuestionSection C · 5 marksEasy

Join each pair using the word in brackets:
(a) I was reading. The light went off. (while)
(b) She went to bed. She had finished her work. (after)
(c) He ran fast. He might catch the bus. (so that)
(d) You will fail. You do not work hard. (unless)
(e) The man is old. He is very active. (although)

Answer: (a) While I was reading, the light went off. (b) She went to bed after she had finished her work. (c) He ran fast so that he might catch the bus. (d) You will fail unless you work hard. (e) Although the man is old, he is very active.

Show the explanation
  1. while / after: time; so that: purpose; unless: condition (= if … not, so "do not" disappears); although: concession.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: rule:1a

17QuestionSection C · 5 marksMedium

Write (S), (D) or (X) against each sentence and give a reason:
(a) When the bell rang, the students went out.
(b) The bell rang and the students went out.
(c) After the bell, the students went out.
(d) The students went out because the bell rang.
(e) The bell rang, so the students went out.

Answer: (a) X (b) D (c) S (d) X (e) D

Show the explanation
  1. (a) and (d) have adverb clauses (time, reason): complex.
  2. (b) and (e) join two independent clauses with "and" / "so": compound.
  3. (c) has one finite verb; "After the bell" is a phrase: simple.
← Back to the question 📖 Section: Sentence types
