1Adverb clauses of time10 examples

They answer when? and begin with when, while, as, before, after, as soon as, since, till / until, by the time, whenever, once.

  1. When the rain stopped, we went out.

    when at the time that

  2. I read a book while I was waiting for the bus.

    while during the time that

  3. Switch off the fan before you leave the room.

    before earlier than

  4. After he had finished his dinner, he went for a walk.

    after past perfect for the earlier action

  5. As soon as the teacher entered, the class became silent.

    as soon as immediately after

  6. I have known her since we were children.

    since from a point in the past

  7. Wait here until I come back.

    until present simple for the future

  8. By the time we reached the station, the train had left.

    by the time not later than

  9. She smiles whenever she sees me.

    whenever every time that

  10. As I was crossing the road, I saw an accident.

    as during the time that

2Adverb clauses of place and manner10 examples

Place clauses answer where? (where, wherever); manner clauses answer how? (as, as if, as though).

  1. Put the book back where you found it.

    place — where in the same place

  2. My dog follows me wherever I go.

    place — wherever to any place

  3. Where there is a will, there is a way.

    place — where a proverb

  4. Sit where you can see the board clearly.

    place — where answers where?

  5. You may go wherever you like.

    place — wherever any place you choose

  6. Do the sum as I showed you.

    manner — as in the way that

  7. He talks as if he knows everything.

    manner — as if in a way that suggests

  8. She looked at me as though she had seen a ghost.

    manner — as though an imagined comparison

  9. Write the answers as the instructions say.

    manner — as answers how?

  10. Leave the room as you found it.

    manner — as in the same condition

3Adverb clauses of purpose10 examples

They answer for what purpose? and begin with so that, in order that, lest.

  1. He works hard so that he can support his family.

    so that + can present purpose

  2. I wrote it down so that I would not forget.

    so that + would past purpose

  3. She spoke loudly so that everyone could hear her.

    so that + could past purpose

  4. We left early so that we might catch the first bus.

    so that + might formal past purpose

  5. The government built dams in order that farmers may get water.

    in order that formal

  6. Speak slowly so that the children understand you.

    so that + present present simple also possible

  7. Walk carefully lest you should fall.

    lest = so that you do not fall

  8. He saved money so that he could buy a bicycle.

    so that + could past purpose

  9. Lock the door so that no one can get in.

    so that + can negative purpose with no one

  10. She practised every day in order that she might win the prize.

    in order that formal past purpose

4Adverb clauses of reason10 examples

They answer why? and begin with because, as, since, now that.

  1. I stayed at home because I had a headache.

    because the strongest reason word

  2. As it was getting dark, we turned back.

    as a reason the listener may know

  3. Since you are tired, you should rest.

    since = as

  4. He was punished because he told a lie.

    because answers why?

  5. Now that the exams are over, we can relax.

    now that because now

  6. We could not play as the ground was wet.

    as reason after the main clause

  7. She did not buy the dress because it was too expensive.

    because answers why not?

  8. Since nobody came, the meeting was cancelled.

    since a reason (not time here)

  9. The roads are flooded because it rained all night.

    because cause and effect

  10. As he was ill, he could not attend school.

    as answers why?

5Adverb clauses of comparison10 examples

They begin with than or as … as and often leave out words that are understood.

  1. She runs faster than I do.

    than comparative + than

  2. The film was better than we had expected.

    than past perfect for the earlier idea

  3. He is taller than his father is.

    than is can be left out

  4. This box is as heavy as that one is.

    as … as equal comparison

  5. Rina is not so clever as her sister.

    not so … as = not as … as

  6. He spent more money than he earned.

    than more … than

  7. I like tea more than I like coffee.

    than I like can be left out

  8. The exam was easier than I thought.

    than comparative + than

  9. Come as early as you can.

    as … as with an adverb

  10. The weather today is not as hot as it was yesterday.

    not as … as negative comparison

6Adverb clauses of concession (contrast)10 examples

They show a surprising contrast with the main clause and begin with although, though, even though, even if, however, no matter how, whether … or.

  1. Although it was raining, they continued the match.

    although an unexpected result

  2. Though he is rich, he lives simply.

    though = although

  3. She went to school even though she was ill.

    even though stronger than though

  4. Even if you run, you will not catch the bus.

    even if whether or not you run

  5. However hard I try, I cannot solve this sum.

    however + adjective / adverb no matter how

  6. No matter what you say, I will not change my mind.

    no matter what whatever you say

  7. Whether you like it or not, you must take the medicine.

    whether … or not it makes no difference

  8. He failed although he had worked hard.

    although clause after the main clause

  9. Poor though he was, he was honest.

    adjective + though an emphatic word order

  10. Whatever happens, keep calm.

    whatever no matter what

7Conditions: zero and first conditionals10 examples

Zero conditional (present + present) for facts; first conditional (present + will) for a real possibility. unless = if … not.

  1. If you heat water to 100 °C, it boils.

    zero conditional a scientific fact

  2. Plants die if they do not get water.

    zero conditional always true

  3. If you mix red and yellow, you get orange.

    zero conditional if = whenever

  4. If it rains tomorrow, we will stay at home.

    first conditional present simple + will

  5. If you hurry, you will catch the train.

    first conditional a real possibility

  6. You will fail unless you study.

    unless = if you do not study

  7. Unless you water the plants, they will die.

    unless = if you do not water

  8. I will lend you the book provided you return it on Monday.

    provided only if

  9. If he calls, tell him I am out.

    first conditional + imperative an instruction for a real case

  10. Supposing it snows, what will you do?

    supposing imagine that

8Conditions: second and third conditionals10 examples

Second conditional (past + would) for an imagined present; third conditional (had + V3, would have + V3) for a past that did not happen. Inversion drops if.

  1. If I had wings, I would fly to the moon.

    second conditional imaginary — I have no wings

  2. If I were you, I would apologise.

    second conditional were for all persons in formal English

  3. She would buy a car if she had enough money.

    second conditional unreal present

  4. If we lived near the sea, we could swim every day.

    second conditional could = would be able to

  5. Were I rich, I would help the poor.

    second conditional (inversion) = If I were rich

  6. If you had told me earlier, I would have helped you.

    third conditional you did not tell me

  7. If it had not rained, we would have won the match.

    third conditional it did rain

  8. He could have caught the bus if he had left on time.

    third conditional he did not leave on time

  9. Had I known about the test, I would have studied.

    third conditional (inversion) = If I had known

  10. She might have passed if she had worked harder.

    third conditional might = perhaps would

9Simple, compound and complex sentences10 examples

Simple = one clause; compound = two or more independent clauses; complex = one main clause with subordinate clause(s).

  1. The old man walked slowly to the temple.

    simple one finite verb (walked)

  2. Being tired, she went to bed early.

    simple being is non-finite; one finite verb (went)

  3. She was tired, so she went to bed early.

    compound two independent clauses joined by so

  4. He knocked, but nobody answered.

    compound two independent clauses joined by but

  5. I came, I saw, and I conquered.

    compound three independent clauses

  6. She went to bed early because she was tired.

    complex main clause + adverb clause

  7. I know that she is tired.

    complex main clause + noun clause

  8. The girl who sat next to me was tired.

    complex main clause + relative clause

  9. He said that he was hungry and that he wanted food.

    complex two coordinate noun clauses, both subordinate to he said

  10. She opened the letter as soon as it came, and she read it to her mother, who was waiting.

    compound (with subordinate clauses) two independent clauses, each with its own subordinate clause
