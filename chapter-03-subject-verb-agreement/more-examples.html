1The basic rule; there and it10 examples

The verb agrees with its subject in number and person. After an introductory there, it agrees with the noun that follows; it for time, distance and weather is always singular.

  1. My brother is a soldier.

    singular subject third person singular → is

  2. My brothers are soldiers.

    plural subject plural → are

  3. She plays the violin; they play the guitar.

    present simple he / she / it + V-s; plural + V1

  4. I am ready, but you are not.

    first / second person I am, you are

  5. There is a temple on the hill.

    there + singular noun the real subject is a temple

  6. There are many temples in Bhubaneswar.

    there + plural noun the real subject is many temples

  7. There was a lot of noise in the street.

    there + uncountable noun noise is uncountable → was

  8. It is half past seven.

    it for time always singular

  9. It is about three kilometres from here to the station.

    it for distance always singular

  10. It was very cold last night.

    it for weather always singular

2One amount, one idea10 examples

A plural amount of time, distance, weight or money seen as one figure takes a singular verb; two nouns joined by and take a plural verb unless they form one idea.

  1. Ten kilometres is a long way to walk.

    distance as one figure singular verb

  2. Two hours is enough time for this paper.

    time as one figure singular verb

  3. Five hundred rupees is too much for this shirt.

    money as one sum singular verb

  4. Fifty kilos is too heavy for one person to lift.

    weight as one quantity singular verb

  5. Rice and dal is our everyday meal.

    and — one notion a single dish → singular

  6. Slow and steady wins the race.

    and — one idea one quality → singular

  7. The horse and carriage was waiting at the gate.

    and — one object thought of as one thing

  8. Rabi and Rina are twins.

    and — two people plural verb

  9. My pen and my pencil are in the box.

    and — two things plural verb

  10. Three boys and a girl have won prizes.

    and — mixed subject the total subject is plural

3Collective and special nouns10 examples

A collective noun takes a singular verb as one body and a plural verb when we think of its members. police, people, cattle take plural verbs; furniture, food, scenery take singular verbs.

  1. The team has won the trophy.

    collective — one body the team as a whole

  2. The team are putting on their jerseys.

    collective — members each member acts separately (their)

  3. The class was taken to the museum.

    collective — one body singular

  4. The family were arguing among themselves.

    collective — members among themselves shows individuals

  5. The police are searching for the thief.

    police always plural

  6. People are waiting for the bus.

    people plural verb

  7. The cattle were taken to the river.

    cattle plural verb

  8. The furniture in this room is new.

    furniture uncountable → singular

  9. The scenery of Koraput is breathtaking.

    scenery singular verb

  10. Her jewellery was stolen.

    jewellery singular verb

4Find the real subject10 examples

Look past a clause or phrase to the real subject. either of, neither of, none of, each of and one of take singular verbs; no / some agree with the noun after them.

  1. The boy who won the prizes is my neighbour.

    clause between subject = the boy, not prizes

  2. The letters that came this morning are on your desk.

    clause between subject = the letters

  3. Either of the roads leads to the market.

    either of singular verb

  4. Neither of my friends has a car.

    neither of singular verb (formal)

  5. None of the answers is correct.

    none of singular verb (formal)

  6. Each of the players was given a medal.

    each of singular verb

  7. One of my cousins lives in Mumbai.

    one of subject = one, not cousins

  8. No student has brought a calculator.

    no + singular noun singular verb

  9. Some milk is left in the jug, but some eggs are broken.

    some + noun the noun after some decides

  10. The poet and singer is coming today. / The poet and the singer are coming today.

    two titles, one or two people one the → one person (singular); two the → two people (plural)

5Fractions, quantity phrases and -s nouns10 examples

After a fraction or a lot of / most of / some of, the verb agrees with the noun that follows; a crowd of / a herd of take singular verbs; a number of is plural, the number of singular; Physics, news, measles are singular.

  1. Half of the cake has been eaten.

    fraction + singular noun singular verb

  2. Half of the guests have left.

    fraction + plural noun plural verb

  3. A lot of rice was wasted at the feast.

    a lot of + uncountable singular verb

  4. Most of the mangoes are ripe.

    most of + plural noun plural verb

  5. A flock of birds was flying overhead.

    a flock of singular verb in spite of birds

  6. A number of students are absent today.

    a number of = many → plural

  7. The number of students is rising every year.

    the number of the subject is the number → singular

  8. Mathematics is my favourite subject.

    -s noun (subject) singular verb

  9. The news is too good to be true.

    news singular verb

  10. Mumps is common among children.

    disease name singular verb

6Connecting words in the subject10 examples

With with / as well as / together with the verb agrees with the first word; with either … or / neither … nor / not only … but also it agrees with the nearer subject.

  1. The captain, with his players, has reached the ground.

    with verb agrees with the captain

  2. Rina as well as her sisters sings well.

    as well as verb agrees with Rina

  3. The headmaster together with the teachers was present.

    together with verb agrees with the headmaster

  4. The house in addition to its gardens is for sale.

    in addition to verb agrees with the house

  5. Either the teacher or the students are to blame.

    either … or verb agrees with the nearer subject

  6. Either the students or the teacher is to blame.

    either … or nearer subject is singular

  7. Neither my father nor my brothers smoke.

    neither … nor nearer subject is plural

  8. Neither you nor I am going.

    neither … nor verb agrees with I

  9. Not only the parents but also the child was happy.

    not only … but also nearer subject is the child

  10. Not only the manager but also the workers have agreed.

    not only … but also nearer subject is plural

7Pair nouns, classes, names, clauses and relative clauses10 examples

Pair nouns are plural (but a pair of … is singular); the + adjective for a class is plural; a plural title or name is singular; a clause as subject takes a singular verb; in a relative clause the verb agrees with the word before who / which / that.

  1. My spectacles are on the table.

    pair noun plural verb

  2. These trousers need ironing.

    pair noun plural verb

  3. A pair of scissors is in the drawer.

    a pair of singular verb

  4. The poor need our help.

    the + adjective a class of people → plural

  5. The young are full of energy.

    the + adjective plural verb

  6. The United States is a large country.

    plural name, one country singular verb

  7. Great Expectations was written by Charles Dickens.

    book title one book → singular

  8. What you need is a good rest.

    clause as subject singular verb

  9. Whether he will come or not is uncertain.

    clause as subject singular verb

  10. She is one of the girls who have won scholarships.

    relative clause who refers to the girls → plural
