Fill in the blanks with appropriate auxiliary verbs.

(a) Either of the boys _____ won a medal.

Rules used

  • either of / neither of / none of

    either of, neither of, none of + plural noun usually take a singular verb.

    📖 Rule 7 (a)
  1. either of + plural noun takes a singular verb.

Answer: Either of the boys has won a medal.

(b) Neither of those officers _____ efficient.

Rules used (as above)
  • either of / neither of / none of

    either of, neither of, none of + plural noun usually take a singular verb.

    📖 Rule 7 (a)
  1. neither of + plural noun: singular verb.

Answer: Neither of those officers is efficient.

(c) One of his friends _____ able to speak English fluently.

Rules used

  • each of / one of

    each of, one of + plural noun take a singular verb.

    📖 Rule 7 (b)
  1. The subject is 'one', not 'friends'.

Answer: One of his friends is able to speak English fluently.

(d) Some honey _____ left in the pot.

Rules used

  • no / some + noun

    no / some + singular noun → singular verb; + plural noun → plural verb.

    📖 Rule 7 (c)
  1. some + uncountable (singular) noun: singular verb.

Answer: Some honey is left in the pot.

(e) The governor and chancellor of the university _____ been invited to this function.

Rules used

  • Two titles of one person

    When 'and' joins two titles of the same person (one 'the'), the verb is singular; with two persons (the … and the …) it is plural.

    📖 Rule 7 (d)
  1. Only one 'the': both titles belong to the same person.

Answer: The governor and chancellor of the university has been invited to this function.

(f) Three-fourths of the crop _____ damaged by the flood.

Rules used

  • Fractions

    After a fraction the verb agrees with the noun that follows: two-thirds of the acid has …, two-thirds of the apples are …

    📖 Rule 8
  1. After a fraction the verb agrees with 'crop' (singular). ('has been' is also correct.)

Answer: Three-fourths of the crop was damaged by the flood.

(g) A great deal of money _____ spent for his mother’s treatment.

Rules used

  • a lot of / plenty of / a great deal of …

    The verb agrees with the noun that follows the phrase.

    📖 Rule 9 (a)
  1. The noun after 'a great deal of' is 'money' (singular). ('has been' is also correct.)

Answer: A great deal of money was spent for his mother’s treatment.

(h) A whole regiment of volunteers _____ working here.

Rules used

  • a crowd of / a regiment of …

    Phrases like a crowd of, a flock of, a group of, a regiment of usually take a singular verb.

    📖 Rule 9 (b)
  1. 'a regiment of' takes a singular verb although 'volunteers' is plural.

Answer: A whole regiment of volunteers is working here.

(i) The number of scholarships _____ increased this year.

Rules used

  • a number of / the number of

    a number of + plural noun → plural verb; the number of → singular verb.

    📖 Rule 9 (c)
  1. The real subject is 'the number': singular.

Answer: The number of scholarships has increased this year.

(j) These days mumps _____ not at all difficult to cure.

Rules used

  • Nouns ending in -s that are singular

    Statistics, Mathematics, Physics, Economics, News, Measles, Mumps, Advice, Information … take singular verbs.

    📖 Rule 10
  1. 'mumps' (a disease) is singular in use.

Answer: These days mumps is not at all difficult to cure.
