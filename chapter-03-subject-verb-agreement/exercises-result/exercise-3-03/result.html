3Question

Rewrite the sentences choosing the correct alternative given in brackets :

(a) What he says about me (do / does) not affect me.

Rules used

  • A clause as subject

    If the subject is a clause, the verb is singular.

    📖 Rule 11
  1. The subject 'What he says about me' is a clause: singular verb.

Answer: What he says about me does not affect me.

(b) Ram as well as his friends (like / likes) to play football.

Rules used

  • with / as well as / together with

    The verb agrees with the first word of the subject.

    📖 Rule 12 (a)
  1. With 'as well as', the verb agrees with the first word, 'Ram'.

Answer: Ram as well as his friends likes to play football.

(c) Neither rose nor jasmine (was / were) available in the market.

Rules used

  • either … or / neither … nor / not only … but also

    The verb agrees with the nearer subject.

    📖 Rule 12 (b)
  1. With neither … nor, the verb agrees with the nearer subject, 'jasmine' (singular).

Answer: Neither rose nor jasmine was available in the market.

(d) A pair of spectacles (were / was) lying on the bed.

Rules used

  • Pair nouns

    scissors, trousers, spectacles take plural verbs, but 'a pair of …' takes a singular verb.

    📖 Rule 13
  1. 'A pair of …' takes a singular verb.

Answer: A pair of spectacles was lying on the bed.

(e) The rich (lives / live) in affluence.

Rules used

  • The + adjective

    The rich, the poor, the blind (a class of people) take plural verbs.

    📖 Rule 14
  1. 'The rich' means rich people as a class: plural verb.

Answer: The rich live in affluence.

(f) The Canterbury Tales (was / were) written by Chaucer.

Rules used

  • Plural titles and names

    A plural name of one thing (a book, a country) takes a singular verb.

    📖 Rule 15
  1. The title of one book is singular although it looks plural.

Answer: The Canterbury Tales was written by Chaucer.

(g) Six weeks (are / is) a long vacation for students.

Rules used

  • Amounts as one quantity

    A plural number of distance, time, weight or money taken as one quantity takes a singular verb.

    📖 Rule 2
  1. A period of time taken as one quantity.

Answer: Six weeks is a long vacation for students.

(h) Your trousers (is / are) torn.

Rules used (as above)
  • Pair nouns

    scissors, trousers, spectacles take plural verbs, but 'a pair of …' takes a singular verb.

    📖 Rule 13
  1. 'trousers' is a pair noun: plural verb.

Answer: Your trousers are torn.

(i) I am young and strong, (amn’t / aren’t) ?

Note: Question tags are not explained in this chapter; this follows standard usage.
  1. The question tag for 'I am' is 'aren’t I?'; 'amn’t' is not used in standard English.

Answer: I am young and strong, aren’t (I) ?

(j) It is you who (has / have) insulted me.

Rules used

  • Relative clauses

    The verb in a relative clause agrees with the antecedent of who/which/that.

    📖 Rule 16
  1. The antecedent of 'who' is 'you', so the verb agrees with 'you'.

Answer: It is you who have insulted me.

4Question

Choose the correct expression from the brackets and rewrite the sentences :

(a) There (is / are) people waiting to see you.

Rules used

  • Introductory 'there'

    After an introductory 'there' the verb agrees with the noun that follows it (the real subject).

    📖 Rule 1 (a)
  • Nouns that are plural in use

    police, people, poultry, cattle, etc. take plural verbs.

    📖 Rule 4 (b)
  1. The real subject 'people' is plural.

Answer: There are people waiting to see you.

(b) Ours (is / are ) a great nation, (isn’t it / aren’t they) ?

Rules used

  • Subject separated from its verb

    Find the real subject word and make the verb agree with it, not with a nearer noun.

    📖 Rule 6
  1. 'Ours' stands for 'our nation' (singular), so the verb and the tag are singular.

Answer: Ours is a great nation, isn’t it ?

(c) The police (has / have) a difficult job.

Rules used (as above)
  • Nouns that are plural in use

    police, people, poultry, cattle, etc. take plural verbs.

    📖 Rule 4 (b)
  1. 'police' takes a plural verb.

Answer: The police have a difficult job.

(d) A lot of cattle (was / were) grazing in the field.

Rules used

  • a lot of / plenty of / a great deal of …

    The verb agrees with the noun that follows the phrase.

    📖 Rule 9 (a)
  • Nouns that are plural in use

    police, people, poultry, cattle, etc. take plural verbs.

    📖 Rule 4 (b)
  1. After 'a lot of' the verb agrees with 'cattle', which is plural.

Answer: A lot of cattle were grazing in the field.

(e) The youth (is / are) not so sincere.

Rules used

  • Collective nouns

    A collective noun takes a singular verb when it acts as one body, and a plural verb when its members act separately.

    📖 Rule 4 (a)
  1. 'The youth' here means young people in general: plural verb.

Answer: The youth are not so sincere.

(f) The youth (was / were) more serious than his uncle.

Rules used (as above)
  • Collective nouns

    A collective noun takes a singular verb when it acts as one body, and a plural verb when its members act separately.

    📖 Rule 4 (a)
  1. 'The youth' here is one young man ('his uncle'): singular verb.

Answer: The youth was more serious than his uncle.

(g) Ill news (travel / travels) fast.

Rules used

  • Nouns ending in -s that are singular

    Statistics, Mathematics, Physics, Economics, News, Measles, Mumps, Advice, Information … take singular verbs.

    📖 Rule 10
  1. 'news' is singular.

Answer: Ill news travels fast.

(h) Mumps (is / are) very unpleasant.

Rules used (as above)
  • Nouns ending in -s that are singular

    Statistics, Mathematics, Physics, Economics, News, Measles, Mumps, Advice, Information … take singular verbs.

    📖 Rule 10
  1. 'mumps' is singular.

Answer: Mumps is very unpleasant.

(i) Bacteria of the harmful kind (cause / causes) diseases.

Rules used (as above)
  • Subject separated from its verb

    Find the real subject word and make the verb agree with it, not with a nearer noun.

    📖 Rule 6
  1. The subject word is 'bacteria' (plural); 'of the harmful kind' only describes it.

Answer: Bacteria of the harmful kind cause diseases.

(j) There (is / are) a pair of spectacles on that shelf.

Rules used (as above)
  • Introductory 'there'

    After an introductory 'there' the verb agrees with the noun that follows it (the real subject).

    📖 Rule 1 (a)
  • Pair nouns

    scissors, trousers, spectacles take plural verbs, but 'a pair of …' takes a singular verb.

    📖 Rule 13
  1. The real subject is 'a pair of spectacles': singular.

Answer: There is a pair of spectacles on that shelf.
