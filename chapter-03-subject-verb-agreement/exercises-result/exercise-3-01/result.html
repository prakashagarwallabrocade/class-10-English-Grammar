1Question

Choose the correct verbs given in brackets and rewrite the sentences :

(a) There (is / are) plenty of job opportunities for the people of this region.

Rules used

  • Introductory 'there'

    After an introductory 'there' the verb agrees with the noun that follows it (the real subject).

    📖 Rule 1 (a)
  • a lot of / plenty of / a great deal of …

    The verb agrees with the noun that follows the phrase.

    📖 Rule 9 (a)
  1. The real subject after 'There' is 'plenty of job opportunities'; the noun after 'plenty of' is plural.

Answer: There are plenty of job opportunities for the people of this region.

(b) There (was / were) plenty of room for improvement in her work.

Rules used (as above)
  • Introductory 'there'

    After an introductory 'there' the verb agrees with the noun that follows it (the real subject).

    📖 Rule 1 (a)
  • a lot of / plenty of / a great deal of …

    The verb agrees with the noun that follows the phrase.

    📖 Rule 9 (a)
  1. Here 'room' (space, scope) is uncountable, so the verb is singular.

Answer: There was plenty of room for improvement in her work.

(c) Two plus two (is / are) four.

Rules used

  • Amounts as one quantity

    A plural number of distance, time, weight or money taken as one quantity takes a singular verb.

    📖 Rule 2
  1. A sum is one quantity: singular verb.

Answer: Two plus two is four.

(d) Two twos (is / are) four.

Rules used (as above)
  • Amounts as one quantity

    A plural number of distance, time, weight or money taken as one quantity takes a singular verb.

    📖 Rule 2
Note: Some grammars also accept 'Two twos is four' (the result taken as one quantity); 'are' is the usual school answer.
  1. 'Two twos' names two groups of two, so the subject is plural.

Answer: Two twos are four.

(e) seventy kilometres (are / is) a long distance.

Rules used (as above)
  • Amounts as one quantity

    A plural number of distance, time, weight or money taken as one quantity takes a singular verb.

    📖 Rule 2
  1. A distance taken as one figure: singular verb.

Answer: Seventy kilometres is a long distance.

(f) Fish and chips (is / are) not so expensive as chicken curry and rice.

Rules used

  • Two nouns as one idea

    Two nouns joined by 'and' that make one object or notion (one dish, one idea) take a singular verb.

    📖 Rule 3 (a)
  1. 'Fish and chips' is one dish, a single notion.

Answer: Fish and chips is not so expensive as chicken curry and rice.

(g) A crowd (has / have) gathered in the street.

Rules used

  • Collective nouns

    A collective noun takes a singular verb when it acts as one body, and a plural verb when its members act separately.

    📖 Rule 4 (a)
  1. The crowd acts as one body.

Answer: A crowd has gathered in the street.

(h) The assembly (was / were) divided on this issue.

Rules used (as above)
  • Collective nouns

    A collective noun takes a singular verb when it acts as one body, and a plural verb when its members act separately.

    📖 Rule 4 (a)
  1. 'divided' shows the members holding different opinions: plural verb.

Answer: The assembly were divided on this issue.

(i) The police (is / are) chasing the burglar.

Rules used

  • Nouns that are plural in use

    police, people, poultry, cattle, etc. take plural verbs.

    📖 Rule 4 (b)
  1. 'police' always takes a plural verb.

Answer: The police are chasing the burglar.

(j) Her clothing (is / are) waterproof.

Rules used

  • Uncountable collective nouns

    clothing, food, furniture, stationery, cutlery, crockery, jewellery, scenery take singular verbs.

    📖 Rule 5
  1. 'clothing' is uncountable: singular verb.

Answer: Her clothing is waterproof.
